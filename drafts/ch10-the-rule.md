# Chapter Ten

## The Rule

I want to tell you about a marriage.

Not because it is charming, although it is, and not because you have been given a false
version of it and are owed a correction, although you have and you are. I want to tell you
about it because in the second week after the river the two of them had the first disagreement
of their marriage, and the shape of that disagreement is the shape of everything that happens
underneath the world for the next two years, and I was in the room for it.

I will say at the start that I understood what I was watching, and that I was the only one in
the room who did, and that this will be the last time in this account that I am able to say
so without qualifying it.

---

First the correction, since it is short and I enjoy it.

Every child on Earth is taught that Hades took Persephone. That he came up through the ground
and dragged her down and that she is in that place against her will and eats nothing but
pomegranate and grief. There are songs. There is a festival in the north of Krete built
entirely on it.

None of it is true and both of them are extremely tired of it, and she in particular has been
carrying it for four thousand years the way you carry a lie somebody else told about you at
school. She was not taken. She went. She is allergic to pomegranate. And the story was not a
misunderstanding that grew — it was written, deliberately, by Zeus and his wife, in an
afternoon, because a queen of the dead who chose it is a woman with an opinion and a queen of
the dead who was stolen is a warning to daughters.

Hold that. I am going to come back to it, and when I do you will see why the argument they had
in the second week was not, in fact, about a dead man from the Amazon compounds at all.

---

They live in three rooms behind the administrative block and they have a kitchen.

I mention it because everybody who has never been down there assumes a throne room, and
because the whole of the following hour took place at a table with a bowl of something on it
that neither of them touched, and because at one point Hades got up in the middle of a sentence
about the fate of every soul in the world to see whether there was any of the tea left.

I was there because she had asked me a question and I had come down to answer it, and I will
give you the question and my answer at the end because I want you to have the argument first.

---

Here is Hades's position, and I am going to put it fairly, which is not the same as putting it
sympathetically.

Protect him.

Nobody upstairs knows. Four people and a Gorgon and a handler saw it happen and one of them
is a woman standing in a wet coat who has no category. Asphodel is talking, but Asphodel talks
about everything and nothing that gets said in a canteen has ever climbed a mountain. So: take
the man off the rota. Put him somewhere quiet with a job that keeps him out of the water.
Learn what happened — carefully, over years, properly, in a book — and do nothing at all with
it until you know what it is.

He made that case for about twenty minutes and it is a good case. I want to be clear that it
is a good case, because what happened to it afterwards has made people forget that it was one.

He also, and this is the part that sat badly and that I do not think he heard himself do,
made it twice. Once as *protect him* and once as *understand him*, and those are not the same
sentence, and the second one has a table in it and a lamp and a man being asked the same
question in nine different arrangements for an hour, which is what had already been happening
at the Lethe for a fortnight and which Caeneus had not once objected to, because he never does.

---

Here is Persephone's position.

She let him finish. Then she said: "There are two hundred thousand people down there who have
been told to wait two hundred years."

And that was the whole of it. She did not enlarge on it. She has never in her life enlarged
on anything.

I will do the enlarging, because it is my job and because she would not thank me for it.

She has been running that place in practice for a very long time. Not the metaphysics — the
rota. The diver rosters, the intake, the standards, the arguments with Olympus about volume.
When Zeus rang up demanding more divers by the end of the day so that some graffiti on a
monument could be punished, it was Persephone who said that you cannot train a diver in an
afternoon and was told it was crowd control, not art.

Every one of those rosters put somebody in the water. Every one of them was signed by her. She
has spent four thousand years being the competent one in a building whose entire function is a
lie, and being competent at it, and being the reason it ran well.

*Use him* is not ruthlessness. It is a woman who has done the paperwork for a crime finding
out there is a way to undo some of it and being unable to think of a reason to wait.

---

Now. The thing neither of them said, which is what the argument was about.

Ten days before that, in that same building, a living man walked the whole length of the
underworld to ask for his wife back, and Hades received him with real warmth, and refused him,
and the grounds of the refusal were not procedural. He said the dead do not come back. He said
it as the one thing he has instead of a defence — that there are rules, and he keeps them, and
the rules are the entire difference between him and his brother, who has none and does what he
likes.

And then his wife went out into the corridor and countermanded him and sent the man back up
with his wife walking behind him.

They have never had that conversation. There was lightning that week, and a swimming pool, and
a sandwich, and then a woman came out of a river, and the row simply never got had.

So when he says *protect him*, what he is asking for is not custody. He is asking whether there
is still a rule.

Because if a dead man can be walked into a river and used, then the dead come back as policy,
and the sentence he has said to every soul that ever asked him for anything is no longer true,
and it was not true when he said it, and he built the machine that made it not true, on
instruction, and then kept it for four thousand years because there were rules and he kept
them.

And she is not arguing with that. She simply does not think a rule that produced the Nothing
is owed anything.

Neither of them says a word about Orpheus. It is in every sentence and it is not in one of
them, and they are both far too old and far too fond of each other to bring it out on the
table where it would have to be dealt with.

---

I will give you the two moments in it that were not policy.

The first: she said, once, quietly, not as an argument, that she had wondered what the year
would have looked like if somebody had gone up that same night and simply told him — while he
was still frightened, before the Fates and the rationing had a chance to work on him.

He heard that as an opening. I watched him hear it. He said something gentle about how they
had made the right decision on the bank and that he would take it again.

It was not an opening. Persephone does not waste her time on questions with answers, and if
she is turning something over out loud it is because it does not have one, and the correct
response was to sit in it with her and he reached for reassurance instead, which is what he
reaches for, and which is why he built the Frame.

The second: he asked what Caeneus wanted.

He asked it as a trump — I am fairly sure of that, and I have wondered since whether I am being
unkind — because it is the obvious question and nobody had asked it and he had noticed that
nobody had asked it.

She had asked him. That morning.

She said she had put it to him straight: would you do it again. And he said yes, immediately,
without any weighing at all.

And she said she had then asked him how many times.

And he had not understood the question.

Not evaded it. Not been modest about it. He had looked at her and asked her what she meant, and
she had rephrased it twice and he had still not got there, because he does not experience the
thing as a quantity of anything. He went into a river after his mother and he held on to her.
There is no unit.

She reported that flatly and moved on to the next item, and neither of them did anything with
it, and I made a note about it because I make notes, and my note was three words long and said
*mechanism not scalar*, which I was pleased with at the time.

I would like you to keep that exchange. It is the only thing in this chapter you will need
later.

---

Nothing was decided.

That is the correct outcome and it is what makes the room in a fortnight's time the room it
is. He did not concede and she did not press and the two of them washed up and went to bed
having had the first disagreement in four thousand years of marriage, which as far as I can
establish neither of them mentioned again.

What changed was smaller. He stopped saying *understand him* out loud, and started saying
*protect him* on its own, and kept the notebook. And she started scheduling.

That is what she does instead of winning. She did not carry the argument; she put four items
on an agenda for a Tuesday and made sure the people who would carry it were in the room, and
one of them was a woman off the Lethe desk called Ione whose entire contribution was going to
be a complaint about not having a line to give people.

---

Now. Her question, and my answer, since I promised it.

She asked me what Zeus would do if he found out.

And I told her, at some length, and I was right about all of it. I said he would not
investigate, because investigating is what a man does when he thinks he might be wrong. I said
he would not come down, because he has not been down in nine hundred years and coming down
would mean the place was important. I said he would reach for the largest available action and
perform it upward, at the family, as a demonstration — and that the shape of it would be
somebody dying in front of people who could not stop it, and that whoever it was would be
chosen for being unmissed rather than for being guilty.

She wrote it down. She asked two follow-up questions and both of them were good, and I
answered those too.

I have gone back over that afternoon a number of times, and I want to put down what I noticed
and then leave it alone, because it is not the important thing about the second week and I do
not want it to look as though I think it is.

It was the last time anybody in that building asked me what a god would do.

Not the last time I told them. The last time I was asked.

# Chapter Seventeen

## The Bell

I know where everybody is.

That has been the working assumption of this account and I have never stated it because it did
not need stating. I have told you where Zeus is and where his wife is and where his brothers
are; I have told you what a dead man does at six in the morning at a port; I have told you the
inside of a room above a fishmonger's that I had not yet been in. The whole of the last year
comes to you from a man who has been keeping an inventory since before your species had
inventories.

On the twelfth of the month before last, Ariadne of Krete found Daedalus alive in the
labyrinth, four and a half months after her father died.

I did not know he was down there.

I want to be exact about this, because it is going to be tempting for both of us to make it
smaller than it is. It is not that I had him listed as dead and was wrong. It is that he was
not on the list. I had not thought about him since the night he was mauled — not once, in four
and a half months — and when Riddy told me they had found him, my first response, out loud,
was to ask who.

---

Here is what happened, and it took her four and a half months to do it, and she did it in the
evenings.

On the night everything ended in that building, Ari sent staff into the labyrinth to fetch
him. They went. They came back. They said he was not there, which was true, in the sense that
he was not anywhere they could reach, and the search was thorough by the standards of people
searching a building.

The difficulty is that Daedalus built it. That is the whole difficulty and everybody kept
saying it as an explanation and nobody heard it as an instruction. There are corridors down
there that are not on any plan because there is no plan, and there never was one; the plan was
a man, and the man was in it.

He had gone into the parts he made for himself. Wounded, one-handed, in the dark, he had
crawled into a section that the palace's own drawings show as solid rock, and he had stayed
there.

And here is the part I have not been able to put down since. **He did not know.**

He did not know Minos was dead. The last thing he had heard, before the doors opened in the
mechanical way, was Minos hunting him through his own building. He came to on a floor with his
hand gone and no way of knowing how much time had passed, and he did the only thing a man in
that position can do, which is hide from the person who was hunting him, and he went on doing
it for four and a half months after that person had been stabbed to death two floors above.

He could have walked out at any point from about the third week. He would have met people
carrying furniture.

---

Ari did not search. That is what I got wrong when Riddy first told me this, and I said
something admiring about persistence and was corrected.

She rang the bell.

Daedalus made it thirty years ago and it is not a bell exactly, it is a small flat piece of
metal on a wire that makes a particular sound, and its entire purpose was that when Daedalus
went down to feed the boy in the dark, the boy would know it was him and be gentle. Not the
doors. Not the mechanism. Him.

Ari found it in his workshop in the first week and she did not know what it was, and when she
was told what it was she took it down into the labyrinth and rang it, and then went on doing
that, in the evenings, after the business of the day, for four and a half months.

Not calling his name. She tried the name first and stopped, because a man hiding from a hunter
does not come out when he hears his name.

She rang the bell. Because the bell only ever meant one thing in that building and the one
thing it meant was: *it is me, and you are safe.*

Her administration thought she had lost her mind, and said so to each other, and one of them
said it to her.

I have this from Riddy, who was in the outer office for most of it. The president of Krete —
which is what they had started calling her, having tried several other things — was going
underground every evening at seven with a lamp and a piece of metal on a wire, alone,
frequently for two hours, four months after killing her father in a corridor. She had begun
by taking guards. She stopped taking guards in the second month because the guards made noise
and because two of them had asked to be reassigned.

The chamberlain put it to her directly in the fourteenth week. He was not unkind about it. He
said that the man had been dead since the spring, that the staff were talking, that there were
four hundred urgent things upstairs, and that at some point continuing to look becomes a
different activity from looking.

Riddy says Ari agreed with all of it, thanked him, and went down at seven.

On the twelfth of the month before last she was in a corridor she had already tried nine
times, and she rang it, and something moved behind a wall that was not a wall.

---

He had survived on the boy's stores.

I am going to give you this straight because dressing it would be obscene. For thirty years
Daedalus fed Glaucus, and for thirty years he took down more than was needed — consistently,
every time, far more than a man in a cell could eat — and he cached it in the sections he had
built for himself, and he had been doing it so long that nobody at the palace queried the
quantities any more.

He has never given a reason. Riddy asked him once, gently, and he said he supposed he had
always thought there might be a day when he could not get down there.

It was guilt. It was thirty years of a man over-feeding a child he had failed to kill and
then failed to save, and it is the only reason he is alive, and I do not think anybody has
said that to him and I do not think anybody should.

He had water because the labyrinth has water. He had no light after the first month.

And for the last six weeks of it there were people living in the cells above him.

That is the detail I would take out if I were being kind to the reader. Ari had begun
converting the labyrinth — the cells, the holding levels, the parts that are on the drawings —
into housing, because the compounds in the south were emptying and those people had to be
somewhere, and by the end there were the better part of two hundred of them down there with
bedding and a water point and an argument about the rota for it.

He could hear them. He says he could hear them and took them for guards, because guards were
the only thing that had ever been in that building in numbers, and he lay in the dark
listening to families and understood it as a search.

---

I went to see him.

I want that noted, since I have spent this account arriving at places after the event and
being told about them: I went, in person, eight days after they brought him up.

They had him in a room in the west wing with a window and he had asked for the shutters to be
kept half closed, which they were, and which they still are.

He is sixty-something and looks a great deal more than that now. The right hand is gone above
the wrist — it was taken in the first few seconds and he says he does not remember it
happening, which I believe. Most of the sight in that eye. He is very thin in the way that
does not come back quickly.

He was, and this undid me somewhat, apologetic. About the room, about the trouble, about the
fact that Ari had spent four and a half months of evenings underground on his account, which
he described as a waste of her time and appeared to mean.

He asked me whether I had known his son. I said no. He said Icarus would have talked my ear
off, and then did not say anything else about him.

We talked for about an hour, mostly about the building. He is extremely interesting about
buildings and it is the only subject on which he stops apologising.

And at some point — I have gone back over the hour a number of times and I cannot place where,
which tells you how little attention I was paying — he asked me what the place under the world
is built like.

Not what it is. What it is *built like*. Whether it is cut or poured. Whether the river is
channelled or found. Whether the door on the thing they are all so exercised about is a door
in a wall or a wall with a door in it, because those are different problems.

I told him what I knew, which was not much, and I told it as small talk, and he listened the
way he listens, and asked two more questions, and then asked whether the shutters were
bothering me.

I thought a maimed man was making conversation.

---

Ari told him about Glaucus herself, on the first day, before they had cleaned him up, because
she had decided on the way down that he was not going to be given three days of not knowing
and then have it broken to him gently, which is what her father would have arranged.

She told him that Minos killed him. She told him she was there. She told him that she killed
her father about ninety seconds later, and that her mother had said *good*.

Riddy has the account of this from a nurse and I have not asked Ari to confirm it and will
not.

He did not say anything for a while. And then he asked whether the boy had known who she was
at the end.

And she said yes, that he had said the word *twin*, and Daedalus said, "He had about eleven
words," and then said the other ten of them, in order, which is a list he has evidently been
keeping in his head for thirty years and had never before had anyone to give it to.

---

So: I did not know where Daedalus was.

I had told you, for the whole of this account, that I know where everybody is, and it turns
out that what I have is not an inventory. It is a *cast list*. I have never once in four
thousand years kept track of a person who was not doing something in my plan, and I did not
notice the distinction, because everybody I have ever needed to find has been in it.

Daedalus was not in it. He was in the show, as it were, but he was not in the plan — he was a
palace artificer who did a monstrous thing under threat, thirty years ago, and got a life
sentence for it in a building he had built himself.

I noticed, while I was sitting in that room with the shutters half closed, that he is the only
other person I have met who did precisely what he was told and has been paying for it ever
since, and I remember thinking that there was something in that, and that I would come back
to it.

Then he asked me whether I thought the west wing was subsiding, and we talked about that
instead, and I did not come back to it.

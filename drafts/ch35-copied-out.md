# Chapter Thirty-Five

## Copied Out

I said at the end of the last one that this is his and I'd keep out of it, and I meant it, so
what I've done is copy it out.

It's four pages in the fourth volume, dated, in his hand, and he gave me the whole book in the
spring without any conditions on it and has never once asked what I was doing with it. I've
taken nothing out. There are two places where I wanted to and I've left them.

From the line below to the line at the bottom, the *I* is his.

One thing first, because I won't be able to say it afterwards.

There is nobody in it being told any of it.

I went back through the fourth volume looking for one, because it had started bothering me, and
after the port there isn't a single one. Two hundred pages, and he never turns round. That man
spent four thousand years talking to somebody who wasn't there. He told them what to remember
and when to look and how to feel about it, and he was extremely good at it, and it is most of
what he had.

And then one afternoon he turned round to do it and there was nobody, and he never did it
again, and he went on writing anyway for two more years into a room with nothing in it.

He's also gone back to the past tense. It isn't the old one. The old one was the tense a man
uses when he already knows how a thing comes out. This is just the tense you use for something
that happened.

Right. Here.

---

I went up to the house in the week after Poseidon.

It was emptying by then. Hermes had gone in the first days of the year and Aphrodite went after
him and Apollo was packing without saying he was packing, which is done by leaving one bag out
for a fortnight. Ares was in the north end of the building and did not come down for meals.
There was a girl doing the corridors whom I had never seen before and will not see again, and
there were four in the long room at one o'clock where there had been a table's worth in the
autumn, and Athena carved, badly, and nobody remarked on it.

He was out by the pool.

Nobody swims in it. Nobody has ever swum in it; it is not that kind of pool. It was cold enough
that afternoon that the surface had gone flat and grey and looked like a floor, and somebody
had put him in a chair beside it with a blanket over his legs, and the peacock was going up and
down at the far end doing what it does.

He was pleased to see me. He has been pleased to see me for four thousand years through every
arrangement we have ever had, including the one with the chains in it, and it has never once
occurred to him that this was remarkable.

We talked for a while about nothing. He wanted to know whether the girl doing the corridors was
new. I said I thought she was. He said she seemed capable.

Then he asked me where Poseidon had gone.

---

I told him.

I want to set down that I did not decide to. There was no moment in which I weighed it. He
asked in the way he had been asking about the day of the week, pleasantly, with his head back,
and I heard myself begin, and then I did the whole of it.

I told him it was the long room, and that it was a Tuesday, and that his wife was at the head
of the table doing correspondence and I was standing by the door. I told him he stood up and
crossed the room and said her name. I told him Poseidon put an arm out and said all right twice
and did not finish the second one. I told him it took four seconds and that it went through his
hands, the way it went through his hands with Alpheus on the terrace and with his daughter in
the long room.

I told him he did it. I used that construction. *You did it.*

He listened to the whole thing without moving.

And something arrived. I am not going to overstate it and I am not going to pretend I could
read it, but something came up into that face and stayed for a period I would put at four or
five seconds, and it was not confusion.

Then it went.

He said: "Well." And then he said that it was a shame, because Poseidon had been at the house a
lot lately.

---

I put my hands on his neck.

There is no way of writing this that is not exactly what it was, so: I stood up out of my chair
and I went round behind his and I put both hands on his neck, which was warm, because there was
a blanket, and I could feel the whole of him through them, and there was almost nothing there.

There was less in that man by then than there was in the girl doing the corridors. I do not
think it would have taken effort. I think it would have taken about as long as it takes to
decide to stand up, and there was nobody within four hundred yards, and his wife was in the
house and would not have crossed the terrace to stop me if she had seen it out of a window.

I have wanted this for four thousand years. I have wanted it on the rock and off it and in
front of witnesses and alone. I have composed the sentence I would say. I have a version of the
sentence that is nine words long and a version that is one word long and I have carried both of
them past the eagle and past the chains and past the fire and into a house with a swimming pool
nobody swims in, and I had my hands on him, and I could not do it.

And he leaned back into them.

That is what happened. He leaned his head back into my hands and he sighed, the way a man does
when somebody finally does the thing his back has needed for a week, and he said, "Oh, that's
good."

Nobody had touched him since the spring.

I took my hands away and went round and sat down again and he did not comment on any of it.

---

I have been calling it a weakness for longer than there have been countries.

I said it to him to his face on the day I came off the rock: that I had my chance and could not
take it, and that this weakness of mine is the only reason he still rules. I said it as a
confession. I meant it as the worst thing about me. Everyone I have ever told has agreed, and
several of them have been kind about it, and Charon did not say anything at all, which I now
understand was the accurate response.

It is not a weakness. It is the specification.

They did not need a killer. If they had needed a killer they had four thousand years and a
world full of people who would have done it for a house and a title, and the thing about a
killer is that he does it early. A killer kills Zeus in the first century, before there is a
girl in a road in Krete or a boy in a compound or a woman who has arranged to be standing in a
river on a particular day, and then the reservoir stays exactly where it is with a new man
sitting on it, and nothing that has happened in the last two years happens at all.

What they needed was somebody who would spend four thousand years arranging everything else.

And the only way to get four thousand years of arranging out of a man is to make sure he can
never take the short way. Not by stopping him. By choosing one who cannot.

She told me. She told me in a drained swimming pool in the autumn with a pencil behind her ear
and I wrote it down and did not read it: that the reason it had to be Charon is that it had to
be somebody I could not do it to twice.

I heard that as being about Charon.

It was the whole of my job description and I have been carrying it in a notebook since October.

---

So I am not a man who failed to kill Zeus.

I am the instrument that was chosen because it would not fire, and it did not fire, and
everything else got done.

I find I do not know what to do with that. I had expected, if this ever arrived, that it would
be a relief — that there would be some settling in it, the way a house settles. There is not.
It is the same afternoon it was before I understood it and I have the same hands.

I do not know what it is for. There is presumably something it is for. I have stopped being the
person who is told.

---

I stayed until it got dark, which was about four.

He talked. He told me about a man who had disappointed him, and I did not follow which man, and
I do not think he did either. The peacock came down our end and he threw it something.

Then somebody came out to bring him in, and he took hold of the arms of the chair and did not
get up on the first attempt, and the girl doing the corridors put a hand under his elbow and he
thanked her by a name that is not her name.

At the door he turned round.

He said: "Same time tomorrow?"

And I said yes.

---

That's the end of it. The next four pages are about a drainage problem at the port.

He doesn't say anything about the first thing he ever wrote in that book. He starts it, four
volumes back, with an afternoon in that same house on the day he came off the rock, and it ends
with Zeus calling after him — *same time tomorrow?* — and him saying yes without stopping or
turning round, and then a line about how he came back the next day and the day after and had
reasons, and that they were excellent, and that we could weigh them as they arrived.

He doesn't join it up. There's no note. I don't think he noticed.

I noticed, and I've sat with it, and here's my weighing, for what it's worth, which is what
anybody's is.

They were not excellent reasons. He went back the next day and the day after and every day for
two years because that was the only appointment he had, and because a man who has spent four
thousand years being the only person somebody talks to does not stop being that on the
afternoon the chains come off.

And he went up in the week after Poseidon, and got his hands on him, and could not.

Which is the same fact from both ends, and it's the one thing about him I've never been able to
be unkind about, and I've tried.

# Chapter Two

## Gather the Troops

I followed her.

I want to be clear that this was not sentiment and it was not curiosity, although both were
present and I have never been good at separating my reasons. It was that she was the one piece
of the board I could not account for, and I had come down off a rock into a world I had been
managing at a distance for four thousand years, and the very first thing I did with my freedom
— after the drink, and the straw, and the business with the forehead — was to go and find out
where Hera goes when she has nowhere to go.

I knew what she had done. I had heard the call: *Darling, it's Mommy. Gather the troops, and
make up a spare bed.*

I had turned that over for most of the night. I want you to have my working, because I was
wrong in a way that I think is instructive.

I took *the troops* for a manner of speaking. Everyone does; you have probably done it
yourself this year, about a dinner. I assumed she meant the household, whatever household it
was — cousins, hangers-on, whoever a marginal god has about the place — and that what she was
assembling was a *position*, which is what she has always assembled, being the only member of
that family who has ever understood that power is a thing you accumulate quietly in advance.

And I took *darling* and *Mommy* to be a wife's habit rather than information, which was
careless of me, because Hera does not have habits. Hera has instruments.

---

She left Olympus at about four in the morning with two bags.

Two. I counted them, and the two bags have stayed with me since. She is a queen
of four thousand years' standing and she walked out of the house she had spent that entire
period furnishing with a bag in each hand and nothing else at all, and she did not take a
single tacita, which is the detail that should tell you the most and which I did not read
properly at the time.

She did not look back at it. Not at the terrace, not at the burnt ground where the hive had
been, not at the west wing. She went down the long way, on the road, on foot, like a woman
going to a bus.

And she travelled north for nine days, which she did not have to do. She could have been there
in an instant and she went on foot and by cart and once, for two days, on a river barge with
a family who did not know what she was and who she was extremely pleasant to, and I have
concluded that she did it that way because a god who arrives in an instant has announced
herself to everybody who was watching for it, and she had correctly worked out that the only
person in the world still watching for her was her husband.

Nine days is also, and I did not think of this until much later, quite a long time to think
about what you are going to say to a child you have not seen in three hundred years.

---

The compound is four hundred miles north of anywhere I had ever bothered with, in a valley
with one road into it, and the first thing you see is the wall.

It is not a fortification. That is what struck me and what I mistook. It is a stock wall,
waist-high in places, of the kind you put up to keep sheep in and to mark where a thing
begins, and it runs for about two miles around a set of long low buildings with a great deal
of smoke coming out of them, and it is in extremely good repair.

I got there before her, obviously.

I sat on the hill above the valley for most of a day and watched it and I am going to tell you
what I saw, because I saw it clearly and I understood none of it.

I saw men and women carrying things between buildings, in an order. I saw a very large amount
of firewood, stacked in the way you stack when you are not planning to move for some time. I
saw two kitchens going at once. I saw, in a yard on the far side, perhaps forty people doing
something repetitive with their arms which I took to be work of some sort, in lines, and
finishing at a particular time, and going indoors.

I had been chained to a rock for four thousand years and I had lost the habit of the specific.
I looked at forty people moving in lines at a fixed hour, and I thought: *well, that is a
tidy sort of place.*

---

She arrived on the afternoon of the ninth day and she walked up the road.

There is a gate in the wall and it was open, because it is always open, and there was nobody
on it. She went through it and up between the buildings and people looked at her and did not
stop her, and she came to a door in the middle building which is exactly the same as every
other door, and she put the bags down, and she knocked.

And her son opened it himself.

---

Ares is not what you have been told.

I am aware that you have been told something, because they made him a story a very long time
ago and the story is all shouting. What opened the door was a man of about fifty in an apron
with his sleeves rolled up, holding a cloth, who had plainly been doing something in a kitchen
and had answered the door because he was nearest to it.

He looked at his mother.

He said: "You made good time."

That is what he said. Not *what are you doing here*, and not her name, and nothing about three
hundred years. He looked at the bags on the step, and picked them both up, and stood back to
let her in, and said the room was ready and had been ready since the call.

I was, at this point, about ninety feet away behind a wall, and I want to record that I have
never in my life felt more like a man who had turned up to somebody's house uninvited to watch
them through a window, because that is precisely what I was.

---

They talked for four hours and I got about a third of it, and Riddy has since had the rest
from Hera in pieces, and it is not the interesting part.

The interesting part is that at some point in the first hour Hera said something about what
she wanted — a base, a season to think, the household regrouped, the family reassembled around
her instead of around him, all of it perfectly sound and all of it a matter of *position* —
and Ares put down whatever he was holding and said, "Do you want to see them, then?"

And she said, "See whom?"

And he said: "The troops."

---

He took her out through the back of the compound, and the ground goes up, and over the rise
there is a second valley that you cannot see from the road or from the hill I had spent the
day on, and it is full.

Nine hundred.

They were in ranks. Not at attention — they were doing the ordinary things, eating, mending,
one lot playing something with stones — but they were in *ranks*, in the sense that every one
of those nine hundred knew where he stood and could have been where he stood inside a minute,
and had known it for four hundred years.

Dead men. All of them. He has been holding them out of the river since before Krete had a
president, on his own authority, which is a thing nobody has ever queried because nobody in
the administration of the underworld has ever audited an Olympian and because the discrepancy
looks like a clerical fault if you are not looking for nine hundred of anything.

He walked her along the front of it and told her about the boots.

I would like that recorded properly. The god of war walked his mother, who had come to him in
her extremity with two bags, along the front of a standing army she had not known existed, and
what he chose to tell her about was the standing order on footwear — that they are replaced on
a rotation, that he has a man who does nothing else, that a formation is finished the day the
boots are, and that this is the part everybody gets wrong.

She let him. Four hundred yards of boots.

And at the end of it she said, "Ares. Why?"

And he said, "Because it's mine."

---

She wrote to the others in her first week there.

Five letters, in her own hand, which she has not done in living memory for anybody. I know
what was in them in outline because one of the five eventually showed his to somebody who told
Riddy, and it was not a summons and it was not an apology. It was an offer of somewhere to be.
She told them where she was, and who was with her, and that there was room, and that they did
not have to decide anything by coming.

That is, by her standards, nearly tender. It is certainly the best letter Zeus has ever failed
to write.

Nothing came back. Not one of the five answered, and I checked, and I checked again in the
spring because I did not believe it.

At the time I read the silence as caution — a letter from a woman who has just been shut in
a room by the king of the gods is a dangerous thing to be holding — and expected them when
it was safer.

They did come, eventually. Five of them came home in the autumn, to a white house with a
swimming pool in it, when their father had gone back to killing gods with his hands and was
sleeping well for the first time in a century.

Not to the mother who wrote. To the strong one.

I have gone over the letters business more than is good for me and the conclusion I keep
reaching is one I do not like, because it is not about Hera at all. They were not weighing
loyalty and they were not being careful. They were doing what everyone in that family has
always done, which is to go and stand next to whoever is currently able to hurt them, and
their mother had just made the tactical error of writing to them from a position of not being
able to.

---

I did not sleep that night, and I do not sleep at all, so that is a figure of speech, but you
take my point.

I had watched forty men in a yard and thought *tidy*. There were nine hundred more over a rise
I had not walked to, and there had been for four centuries, and the reason I had not walked to
it is that I had already decided what the place was.

Here is the sentence I want to put down and then leave alone.

Zeus rang that boy for years. You heard him do it — *hey, it's Pop, callin' to see if you're
around, and see if you're, I don't know, still mad at me* — and every one of those calls
assumed a grievance, because a grievance is a thing Zeus can understand and can eventually
talk his way out of.

There is no grievance. I have since asked, and Ares was mildly puzzled by the question. He was
not sulking for four hundred years. He was working. He was given war and he did it properly,
which in peacetime means maintenance, and he assumed — for four centuries — that somebody
would eventually want it, and every year nobody did, and he ordered the boots anyway.

His mother said *gather the troops* into a telephone because it is a thing people say.

He went and gathered them.

---

So that is the north, and that is what is in it, and I want you to hold it while we go back
down to a warm country where nothing has happened yet.

Because the useful thing — the thing that made this worth nine days of my new freedom — is not
that Hera has an army. It is that she did not know she had one. Everything she has done in
four thousand years, every arrangement, every quiet accumulation, was done in the belief that
she was working with what there was.

She now knows there is more.

I watched her walk back down through the second valley in front of her son, past nine hundred
men who stood up as she passed without being told to, and she was doing what I have watched
her do at a dinner table for four thousand years, which is counting.

# Chapter Six

## Standing Orders

The priesthood's answer lasted two days.

I want to give you those two days properly, because they are the best thing Ariadne of Krete
ever did and she did it before she had the faintest idea what she was doing, and because
everything that country becomes over the following two years is downstream of a decision she
took about a funeral.

---

They buried her father on the fourth day and her brother on the sixth.

That order was not sentiment and it was not precedence. It was that there is a procedure for
burying a president — there are four hundred years of it, there is a book, there is a man
whose job is the book — and there was no procedure whatsoever for the other thing, and it
took them two days to work out that there was not going to be one.

Consider what they were being asked to do.

The state of Krete had maintained for thirty years that Glaucus, son of Minos, died in
infancy. There is a grave. It is in the palace ground on the north side with a stone over it
and a name cut into the stone, and his mother put a closed coffin into it three decades ago
because she was told the body could not be viewed.

So there is already a grave, with a name on it, and now there is a man.

I would like to record that the palace spent the better part of a day on this as an
administrative question before anybody spoke about it as anything else. Do you open the old
one. Do you cut a second stone with the same name on it, four feet away, which is what the
book would tell you to do if the book had ever imagined it. Do you say anything at all about
the thirty years, or do you put him in quietly and let the stone stand and allow every person
in that city to do their own arithmetic in private.

The chamberlain's recommendation was the last of those, and it was a good recommendation. I
want to be fair to that man, since he is going to be wrong about something more famous later:
it was the correct advice. It cost nothing, it settled everything, and it left the *calling*
in place, which was the only thing holding the roof up that week.

She went out on the sixth day and told them the truth.

---

All of it. Standing on the same steps, in the same coat, two days after reading their words
about her father without adding anything to them.

That her brother had been born first. That the prophecy had said the first child to draw
breath, and that it had not been him, and that her father had not waited to find out. That
Daedalus had been ordered to kill an infant and had not been able to do it. That the boy had
been kept in the dark under that building for thirty years without a name and without
language and had been taught to kill because it turned out he was good at it, and that the
thing they had all been shown on the broadcast that week, the thing in the mask, was him.

And that she had gone down there and taken the mask off and said his name, and that her
father had come in behind her.

She did not say *and I killed him*. That is the one omission and I have thought about it since
and I do not hold it against her. She did not need to. Everyone in that square already knew,
and there is a difference between not saying a thing and denying it, and she has never once in
her life confused the two.

Then they buried him with the full rite, because the rite is the rite. Coin, priest, the
choir, *bless his glorious transition, the Renewal of all human souls*, and the crowd said
*vero* and meant it, and it was a larger crowd than her father had.

They put a coin in his mouth. It is what you do.

I stood at the back of it and watched a woman who had had four minutes of her brother buy him
a passage through the Frame, and I did not say anything, because there was nothing available
to say that would have helped her and because I have been not saying it for four thousand
years and had got good at it.

She will find out what she did in about five weeks, from a dead woman in a borrowed coat. I
am not going to arrange the moment for you when it comes; I am simply telling you now that
the coin went in on the sixth day, so that you have it.

---

Now. What that did to the doctrine.

The priesthood had spent four bad-tempered days establishing that the president was *called*,
in the night, by Olympus, in a manner not given to men to understand. They had said it out
loud, in public, in front of two hundred thousand people, forty-eight hours earlier.

And then his daughter stood in the same place and explained, in detail, with names and dates,
exactly what he had been doing and exactly who had told him to do it, and the answer was not
Olympus in the night. It was a prophecy read wrong thirty years ago by a frightened man
who had been given some advice, and everything after it was administration.

They could not withdraw the *calling*, and that is the whole of it. A thing said on those steps
on a holy day cannot be unsaid by the people who said it; the only instrument they had for
correcting the record was the record, and she had just used it.

So Krete went into the summer holding two accounts of the same fortnight, both of them
official, one of them issued by the priesthood and one by the head of state, and no mechanism
anywhere in that country for deciding between them.

I watched them not decide for three weeks and then I stopped watching, because it was
becoming clear that not deciding was the answer and that it would hold for years. It is
holding now.

---

Her mother was in the house.

Everybody around that woman expected grief. She had been given her son back and had him taken
away in the same hour, at thirty years old, having been told for the whole of that time that
he was in the ground on the north side. When they brought her down to the body in the throne
room she was told that her husband had done it and that her daughter had killed him for it,
and she said *good*, and everybody in that building heard about it within the day and took it
for a mother's verdict.

It was not grief and it was not a verdict. That woman had been grieving Glaucus for thirty
years and was extremely good at it; it was the most practised thing she did. It did not need
to start.

What she had lost was the other thing.

There is an annual waxwork. Daedalus made one every year at the age the boy would have been —
thirty of them now, in a room, and they are not good, and the reason they are not good is that
the man making them had seen the subject that morning and could not use any of it. She
inspected each one and said the nose was wrong, every year, for thirty years, and then she
told her daughter that Glaucus had been perfect and that Ari had come into the world and
filled it with rage.

That is what the waxworks were. They were not a memorial. They were an annual instrument for
saying one sentence to a living child, and the sentence was that the wrong one had lived.

And it is now void. Ari did not smother anybody in her sleep. The wax, the nose, the birthday,
the thirty years of it, all of it stood on a lie told by the man who had married one of them
and fathered the other.

She said *good* and it cost her the only thing she had.

Ari has taken that word as an absolution and has gone on taking it, and I did not correct her,
because she needed something that fortnight and there was nothing else on offer. It was not
one. Nobody in that house has ever said a forgiving thing to that woman's daughter, and an
accusation withdrawn is not an accusation answered.

Her mother asked for one of them. The first one — the one made when the boy would have been
one — and Ari had it carried up, and it is in her mother's room, and it stayed there.

The other twenty-nine are still in the room they were always in. Nobody has decided anything
about them. There is no procedure.

And the commission is a standing order. It goes into the palace accounts every year on the
same date, and it went in that year, on the date, and a clerk brought it up to be signed
because a clerk brings it up to be signed, and Ari looked at it for a while and asked who
would make it now, and nobody had thought about that either.

---

The other business of those weeks was housing, and this is the one I want you to keep.

The Trojan population of Krete lives in compounds in the south, on land nobody else wants,
under a registration system that follows a person for life. That was Minos's arrangement and
it was Poseidon's before that and nobody has reviewed it in a generation.

Ari began emptying them in the third week.

Not out of principle, or not only. There was a practical fact, which is that the compounds
were falling down and two of them had no water that summer, and there was a political one,
which is that she had just stood on those steps and told a full square that her father fed
seven Trojans to a man in a mask and she could not then leave the rest of them in his sheds.

And there was nowhere to put them. That is the whole of the decision and everybody who has
since described it as a gesture was not in Heraklion that month. There is no housing stock in
that city. There is a palace, there are temples, and there is the largest single structure in
the country sitting empty under the palace with cells in it, and holding levels, and water,
and a rota's worth of doors.

She put them in the labyrinth.

The first of them went down in the fourth week — sixty-odd people, families, with bedding and
a water point and an immediate argument about the rota — into the cells of the building in
which her brother had been kept in the dark for thirty years and in which seven of their own
had been killed in a night.

Somebody said this to her. I do not know who and I have not asked. She is reported to have
said that she was aware of it, and that they could have the cells or they could have the
south, and that she would take the cells and would understand anybody who did not.

Most of them took the cells.

I want it on the record that I thought this was sound. It is sound. It is the only available
answer to the actual question and she took it in about a day, and there is a version of the
next two years in which it is the making of her.

It is also, and she could not have known this and neither could I, a sentence that is going
to be handed back to her in a room in that same palace, in the same tone, with two words on
the end of it. I am not going to give you the two words now. You would not feel them yet.

Keep the cells. You will want them three times.

---

One more thing from those weeks and then I will take you back up the mountain.

The compound numbers did not add up.

I looked at them because I look at everything, and the transfers Ari's people were recording
did not account for the emptying. The south was going down faster than she was moving it.
Not dramatically — a few dozen here, a household there, and always from the outlying ones,
and always people who were on the register at an address they had never lived at.

I made a note of it. I made a good note of it: somebody is moving Trojans out of the south
and is doing it competently and is not telling the president of Krete. I was entirely right
about that, and I was pleased with it, and I filed it under *to be established* and went on
to the next thing, which is what a man does when he has four thousand years of practice and a
system.

You will meet him. It takes about seven months.

---

The workshop was still as it had been.

They had gone in on the second day to look for anything of his — Daedalus was missing, and
the search of the labyrinth had come back saying he was not there, and I want to say plainly
that the search was thorough by the standards of people searching a building — and then
nobody had gone in again, because there was nothing in it anybody needed.

Ari went in during the first week and stood in it for a while, which people do.

There is a table with two places set. There are the tools, and the drawings, and a great many
small things in drawers that a man accumulates over thirty years of being a prisoner with a
craft.

She went through the drawers.

In one of them there was a small flat piece of metal on a wire. She picked it up and turned it
over and it made a noise, and she looked at it, and she put it back in the drawer and closed
the drawer and went upstairs, because there was a great deal upstairs.

I was standing in the doorway of that workshop and I saw her do it.

# Chapter Four

## How Did You Do That

I have given you my afternoon. The drink, the straw, the forehead, the ball boys with the
dustpan. I would like to go back over the same few hours from two hundred feet under the world,
because they were the same few hours, and because what happened down there is the only thing
that occurred that day which was of any importance whatsoever.

I was not in the water and I did not see it. I was on a terrace being fond of.

I got there in the fourth hour — as fast as it could be done, given that I had first to hear
of it — and I have the first part from four people who were standing on that bank, and they
agree, and where they don't I have said so.

---

She walked in to prove a point.

That is the thing to hold on to and it is the thing everybody forgets when they tell this now.
She was not making an escape and she was not sacrificing herself; she had spent ten years in a
port in the dark keeping an appointment, and she had come to take her son through the Frame,
because that was the arrangement and it had to be that day, and when he would not have it she
walked into the water in front of him to show him it was safe.

*You see?*

That is what she said. Those are the last words she said before it and I have them from three
of the four.

And he went in after her, which she had not planned for, and which — I want to be careful here
because everybody wants this to be a decision — was not a decision either. He went in after
his mother.

---

What Prue saw, and Prue was nearest: nothing for about two seconds, and then the water going
wrong.

Not violently. She says it went *slow*, which she cannot explain and has stopped trying to,
and that the noise of the place stopped in the way noise stops when a door shuts, and that
Caeneus had hold of his mother's shoulders from behind and had his face turned away, which is
what he does now with every one of them and which nobody has ever taught him.

Medusa was up on the platform and says she saw it from above and that it looked, from there,
like somebody being pulled out of a hole.

And then his mother said *kaos*, once, quietly, in the tone of a woman identifying something
across a room, and it was over, and she was standing in the river breathing.

---

Now. Breathing.

I want to be exact about that word because it has caused an enormous amount of trouble since,
and I contributed to the trouble, and it took Hades three weeks and Daedalus about four
minutes to make me stop.

She was not renewed. Not in the sense that the whole of the following year runs on — not sent
back up into the world as a new life with none of this in her. That is what happens to the
ones they bring out of the Nothing and it is a one-way door and there is no version of it
where you get somebody back.

She was **intercepted**.

She had gone into the Frame and had not come out of the other side of it yet, and he reached
into that and took hold and brought her back the way she went in, whole, with everything still
on her, and she came out standing up in a river she had walked into ninety seconds earlier.

Nobody had a word for it that night, which is why they said *renewed*, which is Hades's word
and is wrong. It has stuck anyway. Most things do.

The distinction matters and I am going to state it once and leave it, because everybody in
this account had to learn it the hard way and several of them learned it at a river's edge in
front of somebody's family: **you can catch a soul on the way through, and you cannot un-make
one that has already gone all the way.** What comes out of the Nothing has to be started
again. What is caught in the Frame is simply caught.

There was exactly one soul in the history of the world in the second condition on that day,
because there was exactly one person who had arranged to be, and she had arranged it ten years
in advance.

---

Hades came down the bank at a run and then stopped and did not know what to do with himself,
and then he went down on his knees in the mud at the edge, which is not a thing he does, and
he said:

"How did you do that?"

And Caeneus said, "Do what?"

And Hades said, "You can renew human souls."

I have that from all four and they agree on every word, including the pause, and I have
thought about the pause more than the sentence.

Because Caeneus was not being modest and he was not being clever. He had gone into the water
after his mother and had done something with his hands and there she was, and the god of death
was kneeling in front of him asking him for a method, and he genuinely did not have one. He
said so, several times, in about nine different ways, over the following hour, to a man who
kept asking the same question in slightly different arrangements, the way you do with a
foreign language.

The last time he answered it that night he said: "I held on to her."

Hades wrote that down. I saw him write it down. It is the first line of a notebook that by the
spring had four hundred pages in it and that Daedalus eventually asked to see and handed back
after ten minutes with the observation that it was a diary rather than a record, which is the
cruellest true thing anybody said to Hades all year.

---

And then there was the practical problem, which nobody in the entire underworld had a
procedure for, which was: what do you do with her.

She is dead. She has been dead ten years. She was buried with a coin — she made sure of that,
she is not a woman who leaves administration to other people — so she is not unresolved, she
is a soul that was entitled to pass and has been prevented, and there is no category for that
and no sector to allocate her to and no bed.

Medusa got her a bed. Of course she did. She got her a bed in the block behind the port and
put her on the strength as a handler, which is the job her son had for ten years, because it
was the only paperwork that would take her and because Medusa had about ninety seconds to
decide and has never once in this account been slow.

She has been in that job since. She is not, by all accounts, especially good at it.

---

The first decision anybody made that night was not to tell Olympus, and it took twelve seconds.

Persephone arrived at about the second hour, walked the length of the bank once, looked at the
woman standing in the shallows in a wet coat, and said: "Nobody says anything upstairs."

Nobody argued. Hades did not argue, which is worth noting, because a year earlier he had gone
up that mountain specifically to tell his brother a true thing about the underworld and had
been put into a swimming pool for it.

I would like to be able to tell you it was a strategy. It was not. It was four people in the
dark deciding not to hand something to a man who had that week burned three women alive and
set fire to a hive because his son loved a cat, and the deciding took no time at all because
there was nothing in it to decide.

What it means, though — and this is the bit I did not see for months — is that the entire
operation was born concealed. Everything that follows, every rota and every letter and every
argument in that office above the port, happens inside a decision taken in twelve seconds on a
riverbank by people who were tired.

Persephone has said since that she would take it again. She has also said that she has
wondered, more than once, what the year would have looked like if somebody had gone up that
same night and simply told him, while he was still frightened, before the Fates and the
rationing had a chance to work on him.

I do not think it would have looked like anything. He would have taken the man and drained the
river. But I notice that she has thought about it, and Persephone does not waste her time on
questions with answers.

---

The other thing that started that night and that nobody decided at all was that Asphodel found
out.

There were perhaps thirty people at the Lethe when it happened and about half of them were
dead and off shift and had come down for the last crossing, which people do, because there is
not a great deal on. By the following evening the story was in the canteens. By the end of the
week it was in every sector, and by the time anybody in the office above the port had begun
to think about containing it there were, on Ione's estimate, fifteen hundred people who could
tell you a version of it, most of them wrong in the same three ways.

The version that travelled was not the one about a god on his knees. Nobody cared about that.

The version that travelled was: **a woman went into the water and came back out.**

That is all it was. Two hundred thousand people who have been told they are waiting two
hundred years for a door, hearing that somebody came back through one. It moved in four days
without a single person organising it, and it was in Asphodel a full year before it was in
Olympus, and everything that happened on that dock in the winter starts here, in a canteen, in
a queue, with somebody's cousin telling it badly.

---

The part I would have led with, if I were still arranging things for you, is the reunion, and
I am putting it here at the bottom because it was not one.

They stood on that bank about six feet apart for most of an hour while gods talked over them.

She did not apologise. He did not ask her to. There was a very great deal available to be said
by either of them — she gave him to the Amazons and stood there while they did it, and then
shot herself and waited a decade, and both of those are, from where she is standing, the same
act of love, and from where he is standing they are not — and none of it got said that night
or, as far as I know, since.

What she said, eventually, was: "Is it done?"

She meant the prophecy. She had come to take him through the Frame because that was the
appointment, and the appointment was the whole of the last ten years of her, and she wanted to
know whether she had kept it.

Nobody could tell her. Persephone tried and stopped. Hades opened his mouth and Medusa
prevented him, which I have privately thought was the single most useful thing anybody did
that week.

And after a while she said, "It had to be today," in the way you repeat an instruction, and
Prue — who did not know any of the background and was being kind — said, gently, that it had
gone midnight a while ago, so it was tomorrow now, actually.

Riddy has since told me that she thinks that is the worst thing that happened to anybody that
night, and Riddy was in the room under the world watching Hades put his hands on a boy, so she
has a basis for comparison.

---

I arrived at about the fourth hour and I was, I am sorry to say, magnificent.

I stood on that platform in front of two gods, a Gorgon, a handler and a man who had just done
the impossible and did not know how, and I told them what it meant. I told them the prophecy
was in motion and that the family would fall and that this was the hinge of four thousand
years of work, and I was right about all of it, and I said it extremely well.

Nobody asked me anything.

Hades asked Caeneus fourteen more questions that night and did not ask me one, and at the time
I put that down to shock, and I went back up before dawn because I had a great deal to do, and
I did not stay to help with the bed.

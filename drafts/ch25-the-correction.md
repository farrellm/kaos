# Chapter Twenty-Five

## The Correction

They are in the municipal baths on the north side, which have been drained since before the
war and are full of pigeons.

I don't know how long they have been there. I don't know whether they arrived after the
poolroom or whether they have always had it and the poolroom was a day out. There is a card
table set up on the tiled floor of the deep end and a lamp running off something that is not
a supply, and the three of them are playing a game with the scoring written on the back of a
tide timetable.

Clotho sees me first, at the top of the steps where the diving boards used to be.

"Oh," she says. "Him."

"Don't get up," says Atropos, who has not.

I come down the steps. The tiles are slick in the shallow end and I am not steady on them
and there is a good deal of laughing about that, and I let it happen, because there is
nothing else available to do with it.

---

I have a thing I have come to say and I say it badly.

I say: I want a correction.

Lachesis looks up. She has a pencil behind her ear and she is wearing, I notice, a watch
that is not the watch, a cheap thing on a plastic strap, and I find that I mind about that
in a way I am not going to be able to explain to anyone.

"A correction," she says.

I say: you came to my door. You told me it was not my fate to kill Zeus. You told me I had
to kill Charon. You told me to regain his trust and submit to the punishment and not break.
You said there was a greater plan.

"I did say all of that."

I say: which part of it was false.

And she puts the pencil down and thinks about it — actually thinks, takes her time, goes
back through it, and I stand in the deep end of a drained municipal pool in front of a card
table and wait to be told.

"None of it," she says.

I say: say it properly.

"None of it was false." She counts it off on her fingers, patiently, the way you would for
a man who has come a long way. "It was not your fate to kill him. It still isn't. You had
to kill Charon. You did. You had to regain the trust and take the punishment and not break,
and you didn't break, not for a day of it, and I have said so to people who asked." She
stops on the fourth finger. "And there is a greater plan."

I say: is there.

"There is a greater plan," she says. "There is a greater plan and it is going extremely
well and you are standing in it."

---

Here is what I came for and did not get.

I came for one sentence with an untruth in it. Any of them. I would have taken the smallest
— a shading, an emphasis, a thing said in a tone that carried more than the words. A man who
has been used is entitled to at least one lie, and the lie is the thing you hold up
afterwards, and you say *there, that is where it was done to me*, and everything on the far
side of it belongs to whoever told it and not to you.

There isn't one.

I have been through it a great many times since the desk and the manifest and the four
o'clock coming in, and I have been through it out loud, and I have been through it in front
of the three of them now, and every sentence she spoke at my door is true and was true and
is still true, and the entire architecture of my last four thousand years is a thing I built
myself out of true materials.

Nobody lied to me. I furnished it.

---

Megaera would have enjoyed this. That is not relevant. I am putting it in because it
occurred to me while I was standing there and I have stopped being able to tell which of the
things that occur to me are for keeping.

---

I ask why she didn't tell me.

I ask it badly too, with my hands out, in the voice of a man who is a long way past
managing his own presentation, and Clotho winces slightly on my behalf, which I find I would
rather she had not.

Lachesis says: "You didn't ask."

The pigeons go over. There is a skylight out at the far end with most of the glass gone and
they come in and out of it all afternoon, and for a moment the only thing in the baths is
that.

I have heard this sentence before. I heard it at a desk, at the port, on the eleventh day of
my freedom, from a man who put down his pen to say it and picked it straight back up, and I
took it home and decided it was grief and told everybody so.

Two of them. Four thousand years apart. Both of them standing there the whole time with the
answer, waiting for the one question, and I did not ask it of either.

I got what I wanted, is the thing. That is what I cannot put down. I wanted to be the man
with the plan, and there was a plan, and I walked into a room where the plan was and I did
not say *whose is it*, because I already had an answer I preferred.

"You could ask now," says Atropos, brightly.

---

So I ask.

I ask whether it was necessary. Any of it. Whether he had to die for it to work.

Lachesis says yes.

I ask which parts were necessary, and she says all of them, and I ask her to be exact, and
she is: the killing was necessary, the oath was not, the mark was useful, the endurance was
the point, and the reason it had to be him is that it had to be someone I could not do it
to twice.

I ask whether it would have gone differently if I had asked at the door.

"No," she says.

I ask — and this is the one, this is the question I have been carrying up and down a plateau
without ever once looking at it — I ask whether she would have told me. At the door. If I
had stood there and said *whose plan is it*.

She says: "Yes."

Not gently. Not cruelly. In the voice you would use for the time.

---

I don't have anything after that.

I stand there for a while. Somebody's pigeon situation continues. Clotho asks whether I want
to sit down and I say no, and then I do sit down, on the tiles, in the deep end, which is a
thing I am aware of doing and cannot construct a reason not to do.

Atropos deals.

They go back to the game. That is what I want on the record about this afternoon, and I
notice that I still want things on the record, which is either the last working part of me or
the first thing that ought to go: they went back to the game. Lachesis takes the pencil from
behind her ear and writes something on the back of the tide timetable and the three of them
argue about whether it counts.

The world does not stop for it. It has never once stopped for it. I have been told this in
two locations now by two entirely different kinds of creature, both of whom went straight
back to their paperwork afterwards, and I am beginning to understand that this is not
callousness, it is simply the shape of the thing.

There is a plan. It is going extremely well.

I am standing in it.

# Chapter Eleven

## Guttering

The first thing Poseidon did after losing every city on Earth was send back his ration
unopened, in the second week, without a note.

You have had that from me already, in a list, as one item among four on a shelf in a west
room. I put it there because it belonged in the account of what Zeus was doing to himself
and I did not at the time think it was about Poseidon at all.

It is worth going back to, because everybody else in that family sent a message with theirs.
Hades's came back with seven words from his wife. Dionysus's went south on a cart and was never
opened and is still, I am told, in a room. Hera's was held and not sent, which is a message of
a kind and the most eloquent of the four.

Poseidon's came back empty-handed. No note, no verbal, no reply to the boy who carried it. He
simply declined to participate and did not tell anybody why, which from that particular god is
so out of character that I should have gone down and looked at him and did not, because I was
busy with a garden and a chart.

---

Now let me do the analysis I did do, because it was rigorous and I stand by every step of it
and it produced entirely the wrong country.

Poseidon is the most self-interested creature in this story. I want to be careful with that,
because it is usually said as an insult and I do not mean it as one; it is a description of a
faculty, and his is unusually clean. He has never once been confused about what he wanted.
Four thousand years of appetite with no theory on top of it, and no cruelty in it either —
he drowned a servant for something to do and it did not occur to him afterwards to enjoy it
or to be sorry, which is worse than either and is also, in its way, restful.

Such a creature, stripped of everything, does one of three things. I wrote them down.

**He rebuilds.** He goes to whoever will have him and sells the only asset he has left, which
is himself: a god with a portfolio and no work, available. There are governments on that coast
who would take him tomorrow and ask no questions, and he knows every one of them, and he has
had lunch with all of them.

**He waits it out.** He goes somewhere warm and pleasant and he is charming for as long as it
takes, on the sound principle that his brother's rages have a half-life and that a man who is
not in the room is not the current problem. He has done this before. It has worked before.

**He goes back.** He turns up on that terrace inside a month, and he is contrite, and he is
funny about being contrite, and by the second drink it is a story they tell.

Those are the three and I have gone over them since and there is not a fourth. If you had
given me those weeks again with the same information I would have written the same list.

He went north.

---

He arrived at Ares's gate in the seventh week.

He did not send ahead. He came the long way and on foot for the last part of it, which I
noticed and filed under fatigue, and he arrived in the middle of an afternoon at a waist-high
stock wall with an open gate in it and nobody on the gate, and he stood in the road for a
while.

I want to say what I think he had prepared, and I am guessing, and I will flag it when I stop
guessing.

I think he had an entrance. He has done them all his life. There would have been a way of
coming through that gate that made the whole of the previous month into an anecdote, and a
line for Hera that acknowledged the position without conceding it, and a second line for the
son he had never met.

What happened is that a man of about fifty came out of the middle building in an apron with
his sleeves rolled up, because he was nearest, and looked at him and said:

"Have you eaten?"

---

That is what he says. He said it to his mother and he said it to Poseidon and about six weeks
later he said it to me, in the same tone, from about the same distance, and it is not a
courtesy and it is not a test.

It is the first item.

I had that place wrong in the summer and I have said so — I sat on a hill and watched forty
people move in lines at a fixed hour and concluded it was tidy, and there were nine hundred men
over a rise I had not walked to. I would like to record that I have now got it right in the
other direction, because the correction is not *there was an army*. Anybody can see an army
once somebody walks them along the front of it.

The correction is that there is one principle in that compound and it runs the whole of it.

A thing arrives and you maintain it. That is all. Boots are replaced on a rotation because a
formation is finished the day the boots are; firewood is stacked in the summer because it is
wet in the winter; nine hundred dead men are held out of a river for four centuries because
they are his and you do not let a thing you have go to pieces. And when a person turns up at
your gate, you feed him, and then — this is the part — **you find him something to do**,
because a man with nothing to do goes off, in exactly the way a wall goes off, and it is easier
to prevent than to repair.

I have spent two thousand years developing that as a technique. I have four rules and I gave
you all four of them, at length, in a chapter I was pleased with, and the fourth is the one
that matters and I regard it as the finest thing I know.

He does not have a technique. He has never articulated it and would not know what you meant.
He runs a farm.

---

The spare bed was taken.

I do not know whether Poseidon knew that before he came, and I have thought about it a good
deal, because the two readings are quite different and I cannot get underneath either of them.
There is one room in that building that was made up in advance, on a telephone call, before
anybody had left anywhere — *gather the troops, and make up a spare bed* — and it is Hera's,
and she had been in it five weeks.

What Ares did about it took him under a minute and was not awkward, because he is not capable
of being awkward about accommodation. There is a room over the second kitchen with a
window in the roof that had been holding sacks. Two of the household had it clear by the
evening and a bed in it by the next day, and it is warm because it is over a kitchen, and it
is the best room in that compound and everybody there except Poseidon knew that.

He was in it for a year.

---

Here is the thing about the north that I did not appreciate until I had been going up there
for a couple of months, and it is the answer to a question I had not thought to ask.

There is no sea.

It is four hundred miles inland in a valley with one road into it. The nearest salt water is a
three-day journey and nobody in that compound has any business with it; they are not fishermen
and they do not trade by ship and the whole economy of the place is grass, timber, two kitchens
and an army that has not been used. In the entire arrangement there is no task, no problem, no
argument and no shortage that the god of the sea has any bearing on whatsoever.

Nobody asked him for anything.

I want to sit on that for a second, because it is easy to read it as a snub and it was not one.
Nobody was excluding him. There was simply nothing, in that valley, that he was for — and it
is a working household, and everybody in it does something, and the doing is how they know each
other. Forty people who all have a job and one enormous handsome creature who has none.

He was pleasant about it for about a fortnight. He is good at rooms; he was funny at the table
and he was interested in people and the household liked him, and they went on liking him for
the whole of the year and were sorry when he left.

And then the fortnight ended, and I watched a god begin to walk about a farm.

---

Ares gave him the water.

It is not a portfolio and it was not offered as a joke, and if it had been offered as a joke
that man could not have delivered it, because I have never seen him make one. The compound
takes its water off the roofs into butts and down into a cistern cut under the yard, and the
system is older than the buildings and had been silting for a decade and was losing a
quantity every winter that Ares knew to within a barrel, because of course he did.

So: the butts, the runs, the cistern, and a rota of two men to help.

Poseidon did it. That is the whole of what I have to report and it took him most of a year.
He had the cistern out and re-lined by the spring. He argued about the guttering on the long
barn for a month with a man called Perdikkas and lost. He was, by every account I have, neither
good nor bad at it and he did not once complain and he did not once make anything of the
comedy of it, which frankly was available.

God of the sea. Twenty-six barrels and a hole under a yard.

And I would like to put down, plainly, that this is not humiliation and I have been careful not
to write it as one, because everybody I have described it to since has heard it as one and has
laughed in the wrong place. Nobody in that valley was making a point. There was a job that
needed doing and a person standing about, and the man who runs the place put the two together
in the way he puts boots on a rotation, and did not think about it again.

---

Hera was in the house the whole time.

You will want something from me about that and I have very little. They were under one roof
for a year. They took most of their meals at the same table, in a household of forty, with her
son at the head of it. I was in and out of that building for the whole of it and I never once
saw anything I could have written down.

I do not think it was a performance of discretion. I think — and this is as far as I will go —
that whatever that arrangement had been, it had been a thing conducted in a particular building
under a particular man, and the building was four hundred miles away now and the man was in it
alone rationing bottles to people who were not there, and the arrangement did not travel.

She was extremely occupied. She had written five letters and had nothing back and was
recalculating, and she was counting, which is what she does. He was doing the guttering.

---

So. What was he for.

I have been asked this since, by people who assume that a god of the sea installed for a year
in the household of a rival god's army was a piece on a board, and I have had to tell all of
them the same thing, which is that he was not for anything. Nothing was traded. He never
offered Hera an alliance and she never asked him for one and there is no version of that year
in which either of them tried. He was not a hostage, because nobody wanted him. He was not an
asset, because there was no market.

He was a man in a spare room over a kitchen who saw to the water.

And I will be honest with you about the state of my understanding at the time, because I have
promised you that and because it is going to matter later.

I could not account for it.

I had three readings and every one of them was sound and not one of them survived a week. He
did not sell himself, and there were buyers. He did not go somewhere warm. He did not go back
to the terrace, and going back to that terrace was, on the arithmetic available to him, the
correct move — it cost him an afternoon of contrition and it returned him a city and a ration
and a brother, and he did not do it, and he has never done a thing that cost him more than it
returned in four thousand years.

So there is a piece of this that I do not have.

I want that on the record, because I noticed it at the time and said so to myself in a valley
in the autumn, and then I did the other thing, which is that I decided the piece was small.

That is the whole of my error and it is not an error of reasoning. Everything I concluded from
what I had was correct. I simply took a hole in an account of a minor god's living arrangements
and weighed it against a rationed river and a chart and a woman counting, and I put it at the
bottom of the list, and I did not go back to it for a year and a half — and when it did come to
me it came from Dionysus, not making a point, in a completely different spring.

---

One last thing and then we will go back down.

In the seventh week, when he arrived, I made a note that he had not been to the coast.

He had been dispossessed for six weeks by then and he had not been near salt water once, which
for that god is like a man not going home. I put it down to sulking. I wrote the word.

And I want you to notice — because I did not, for a year and a half, and it was in front of me
in my own handwriting — that in all six of those weeks, and in all the weeks after them, and
for the whole of the year he spent four hundred miles inland with his sleeves rolled up
arguing about a downpipe,

nothing whatsoever happened to the sea.

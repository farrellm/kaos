# Olympus — The Divine Power Structure

How the gods are organized, what they actually do all day, and where the fault lines
run. See also [02-COSMOLOGY.md](02-COSMOLOGY.md) for the metaphysics and
[01-CHARACTERS.md](01-CHARACTERS.md) for the people.

---

## The physical place

Zeus's Olympus is a white modernist villa complex with a swimming pool, a terrace, a
long dining table, and a view down onto the Earth. There is a peacock. There is a skeet
range where Zeus shoots clays — and, when sufficiently agitated, staff. There is a
television, brought out and positioned by hand for the Minos broadcast. There are
barbecues, and lamb, and salmon for the one guest who doesn't eat lamb.

The **Meander** runs through it: a divine structure, and the source of the water every
god drinks. Prometheus's rule of thumb — as long as it flows, Zeus reigns.

**Hera's bathroom** is forbidden to Zeus by oath. Inside it is a cabinet of drawers
holding the tacitas' tongues, replayable on demand.

**The hive** holds Zeus's former lovers, transformed into bees by Hera. Dionysus visits
his mother there and does not know which bee she is. Zeus burns them all in Episode 8.

**Prometheus's rock** is on a plateau within eagle-flight and shouting distance. Zeus
visits when he needs reassurance, which is often.

## The staff

**Ball boys.** Anonymous young men who fetch, carry, serve, and load the skeet trap.
Zeus shoots at least three of them dead in Episode 4 over the missing watch, interrogating
each first. Hera's complaint about the killings is that she had to make her own coffee.
They are also the enforcement of Zeus's ban on the word "prophecy."

**The tacitas.** Not staff exactly — mortal women who cut out their own tongues in
service to Hera and receive human confessions in booths on Earth. Their vow of silence is
to Hera; their tongues are Hera's property, and the confessions are Hera's power supply.
Zeus's violation of them is what nearly ends the marriage in Episode 5.

## The hierarchy

```
                    ZEUS  (king; controls the Meander)
                      |
     +----------------+----------------+
     |                |                |
   HERA           POSEIDON           HADES
 (queen;         (the sea;         (death; the
  tacitas;        Krete and         underworld;
  confession)     cities)           built the Frame)
                                       |
                                   PERSEPHONE
                                   (runs it in practice)

   DIONYSUS — full god by Zeus's decree, no portfolio, wants one
   ARES · ATHENA · APOLLO · APHRODITE · ARTEMIS · HERMES — estranged, unseen
```

**Sibling structure.** Zeus, Hera, Poseidon and Hades are siblings; Zeus and Hera are also
married. Zeus assigned the portfolios, and Hades has resented his ever since — Zeus's
Episode 6 taunt is that Hades never liked being given death and always felt he deserved
more, and that he is the runt who belongs down in the dark.

**Rank is not fixed; favor is.** What matters is who is currently in Zeus's good graces.
Dionysus goes from thrown-through-a-table to promised "more power" to disowned within four
days, on the strength of a watch.

## What a "promotion" means

Dionysus's request in Episode 1 is for a real portfolio: love, war, wisdom — something
serious, something with influence. Hera's answer is that there's nothing wrong with being
slightly lightweight and everyone has their place. Zeus's answer, once flattered, is that
a promotion simply means more power, and that power is repaid in loyalty.

The joke underneath: Dionysus frames it to a mortal vendor as wanting to move into HR. He
is the only god who wants to work *with* humans rather than on them.

## The confession economy

Humans visit tacita booths to divulge their darkest thoughts. They believe it is comfort
offered by a wordless servant of Hera. In fact the confessions are kept by Hera; they give
her power and, as Prometheus puts it, soothe her absent heart.

This is a genuine intelligence network — Hera hears the private truth of the mortal world
and Zeus does not. It is also the season's most efficient irony: Zeus finally breaks his
oath and listens, and what he hears is his own wife's voice in another woman's fantasy.
Hera's counter-argument, that he heard a human woman imagining she was Hera, is almost
certainly false and completely effective.

By the end of Episode 8, Hera has been confined and her bees destroyed, but the tacita
network itself is not shown dismantled — she offered to take the tacitas with her when
Poseidon proposed they run.

## Poseidon's portfolio

The sea, and the cities. His management style is direct: he installs presidents, tells
them the arrangement is a loan, and reminds them physically when they forget. Minos of
Krete is his. The dangled prize is immortality for sufficient service.

He is lazy, which is his standing charge from Zeus — the desecrated monument is treated as
evidence that Poseidon isn't keeping his city in order. In Episode 8 Zeus takes every city
from him, including the City of the Dead, meaning Zeus now personally holds Earth's
administration and the underworld's.

## What the gods actually do

- **Weather and disaster** on request or on impulse. Zeus performs storms for Olympia Day
  and, later, executes "phase one" — earthquakes, floods, fire — as a mass demonstration.
- **Myth-making.** Zeus's "phase two," and the older, larger project: writing the stories
  humans live inside. Hera claims joint authorship of the universe as they saw fit. Athena
  is phoned specifically about myth-making work.
- **Answering prayers**, barely. Zeus listens to them in Hera's bathroom the way one
  listens to talk radio, and finds the confessions of crimes more entertaining than the
  pleas.
- **Reproduction on Earth.** Zeus keeps mortal children "cooking" as replacements for the
  ones who disappoint him. Hera systematically kills the infants and transforms the
  mothers.

## Standing rules

1. Gods are only visible to humans if they choose to be.
2. Gods do not bleed and do not die — the season's most-repeated assertion, and the one
   its final act calls into question.
3. Gods do not bring back the dead. Broken by Persephone in Episode 7.
4. Zeus's oath: he may not touch the tacitas. Broken in Episode 5.
5. Hera's oath: marriage and loyalty. Its status is deliberately left ambiguous — she
   denies the affair to Zeus's face while conducting it.
6. The Meander is Zeus's to distribute. In Episode 8 he begins distributing it as a
   discipline, which is the first time divinity itself is used as a leash inside the
   family.

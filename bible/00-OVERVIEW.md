## Premise

The Greek gods are real, contemporary, and running a protection racket. Zeus lives
in a white villa on Olympus with a swimming pool, a peacock, a skeet range, and a
staff of terrified ball boys. Humans worship on schedule, confess into booths,
volunteer for sacrifice, and are buried with a coin so their souls can pass
through the Frame and be *renewed* into a better next life.

None of that is true. Renewal is a lie. The Frame does not recycle souls into the
world; it harvests them. Zeus discovered as a mortal that killing his father
Kronos let him absorb the man's soul, and that the power was intoxicating. He
industrialized the discovery. The gods drink liquidized human souls — Meander
water — to stay immortal. Every temple, every festival, every consenting sacrifice
is infrastructure for a supply chain.

Prometheus, chained to a rock and having his liver eaten daily by an eagle, has
spent thousands of years arranging the counter-move. He narrates the season
directly to the audience. His plan requires three humans and a prophecy — the same
prophecy, word for word, given to all three:

> **"A line appears, the order wanes, the family falls, and kaos reigns."**

The three are Eurydice ("Riddy") of Krete, Caeneus of the Amazons (ten years dead),
and Ariadne ("Ari"), daughter of President Minos. None of them knows what they are
for. By the end of Season 1, the prophecy is visibly in motion and Prometheus has
lost control of it.

## Tone

Tragedy played as workplace comedy, then abruptly played as tragedy again. The
divine is bureaucratic and petty; the human is small and enormous. Casual profanity
in the mouths of gods. Anachronism used flatly and without a wink — voicemail,
answering machines, fax machines, skeet shooting, a game show, corporate onboarding
videos. Needle-drops of recognizable pop and standards against mythic imagery.
Violence arrives without warning and is not softened. See
[09-VOICE-AND-STYLE.md](09-VOICE-AND-STYLE.md).

## The engine

Zeus finds a wrinkle on his forehead. That is "a line appears." Everything else
proceeds from a paranoid god deciding a prophecy has begun — and, in trying to
outrun it, doing every single thing required to bring it about. The Fates tell him
so explicitly in Episode 8: the possibility exists, but without his belief it
cannot come to pass.

Meanwhile the three prophesied humans converge on the truth from three directions:
Riddy and Caeneus discover the Nothing from inside the underworld; Ari discovers
her father's crime and the gods' hand in it from inside the palace of Krete.

## Where Season 2 begins

Six pressures are already loaded:

1. **Zeus alone.** He has destroyed the Fates (he thinks), rationed the Meander,
   burned Hera's bees, seized every city, and driven out or humiliated every ally.
   His court is now a hostage situation.
2. **A conspiracy of gods.** Hera is calling in troops. Poseidon is dispossessed
   and in love with her. Dionysus is with the Trojans. Hades and Persephone are
   working to undo the Frame from below.
3. **Caeneus can renew souls.** The gods' monopoly on the machinery of death has a
   hole in it, and Hades saw it happen.
4. **Riddy is alive and carrying the truth**, told by Cassandra to go to Ari and set
   the living free while Caeneus does the same for the dead.
5. **Ari rules Krete** and has publicly refused to rule for the gods.
6. **Prometheus is free** — and the Fates' parting line was that what he does with
   his freedom is up to him, which is not the same as it being what he planned.

Every open question is catalogued in [05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md).

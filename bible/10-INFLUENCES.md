# Influences — Who Could Write This, and What to Steal

Companion to [09-VOICE-AND-STYLE.md](09-VOICE-AND-STYLE.md). That file describes how
*KAOS* sounds; this one names working authors whose solutions to the same problems are
worth studying before drafting the Season 2 novelization.

Read this as a list of models, not a shortlist of hires. The useful question for each
entry is *what specific technical problem has this writer already solved at novel
length?*

---

## The four constraints

Most candidates fail on one of these, and failing any one of them produces a different
book than the show.

1. **A participant-narrator with an agenda, who must be structurally demoted.**
   Prometheus is not a framing device. He is a character who believes he is the author of
   events and is revealed in the final act to have been an instrument — see
   [05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md) O2 and O3. This is whole-novel architecture, not a
   voice choice: the prose has to carry his authority and then withdraw it.

2. **Comedy and atrocity in the same sentence, with no tonal bridge.**
   Zeus shoots a ball boy between skeet clays and complains about the coffee. The comedy
   is not relief from the horror; it is the horror seen from the position of power. Lose
   the comedy and the violence becomes unbearable rather than shocking; lose the violence
   and it is a sitcom about gods.

3. **Politics carried in plot, not speeches.**
   Refugee segregation, client-state authoritarianism, spectacle as pacification, and
   state religion as literal extraction infrastructure. See
   [08-EARTH-POLITICS.md](08-EARTH-POLITICS.md). Nobody in the show explains the system;
   they live inside it and it kills them.

4. **Violence that arrives without warning and is refused elegy.**
   A truck, a crossbow, a knife in a corridor, a kitten. The show does not linger and does
   not pause. The grief arrives several scenes later, displaced.

**Plus two competencies:** myth fluency deep enough to break the myths on purpose, and the
ability to write Caeneus's transition as character rather than as subject matter.

## First calls

### Claire North — the narrator architecture
The *Ithaca* trilogy (*Ithaca*, *House of Odysseus*, *The Last Song of Penelope*) is
narrated by goddesses in first person, each with a grievance, an agenda, and an explicit
awareness of what she is choosing not to tell you. Direct address, editorializing,
partisanship, and a narrator whose power over the story is itself part of the plot.

That is constraint 1, already solved once at length. North also writes genuine political
machinery — succession, occupation, client kingship, the administration of an island
nobody is allowed to say is leaderless — and she kills people without ceremony.

**Steal:** how a divine narrator can be intimate and unreliable at once without the prose
winking.

### Natalie Haynes — the tone
*Stone Blind* runs an intrusive, sardonic, openly partisan narrative voice that judges
gods out loud and refuses to be impressed by them. Haynes is a stand-up comedian by
trade, so the comedy is structural rather than decorative, and the anger underneath it —
about who is permitted to narrate a myth and who is only ever narrated — is exactly the
show's politics.

**Weakness to compensate for:** her violence is more reported than felt. KAOS needs it
felt.

**Steal:** the register of a narrator who finds the gods ridiculous and dangerous in the
same clause.

### Marlon James — the violence and the collapse
*Black Leopard, Red Wolf* is told by a man giving his version to an interrogator, and its
late move is the disintegration of his authority — the reader's discovery that the telling
was shaped throughout. Violence is extreme, unaestheticized and consequential; myth is
treated as a live political system rather than set dressing.

**Risk:** maximalism. KAOS is fast and short-scened; James is neither.

**Steal:** how to stage the moment a narrator stops being able to hold the story.

### Percival Everett — the comic rage
*James* performs precisely the maneuver KAOS performs: reassign narration to the person
the original story silenced, and let the comedy carry the fury. The best prose on this
list, and the clearest demonstration that a sentence can be funny and murderous
simultaneously.

**Caveat:** wrong register for a three-realm fantasy ensemble. A model for line-level
attack, not for structure.

### Charlie Covell — the honest answer
The showrunner writes this voice natively and knows where Season 2 was going. Named here
because any list that omits them is being coy.

## By strand

| Strand | Model | What to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Olympus as a workplace; the underworld canteen | **George Saunders**, *Lincoln in the Bardo* | Afterlife bureaucracy in which the comedy and the grief are made of the same material |
| Asphodel; the unresolved dead | **Margaret Atwood**, *The Penelopiad* | A dead narrator with a score to settle, in flat modern idiom, unimpressed by her own myth |
| Troy — Hecuba, Andromache, Cassandra | **Pat Barker**, *The Silence of the Girls*, *The Women of Troy* | Occupation and its aftermath; the refusal of consolation; women who survived and are tired |
| Krete, the labyrinth, the executions | **Joe Abercrombie** | Sudden violence, black comedy, political cynicism, and ensemble cross-cutting that never loses a thread |
| Caeneus | **Torrey Peters**, *Detransition, Baby* | Comedy and cruelty about identity in the same breath, with no uplift and no explanation offered to the reader |
| Gods sustained by belief | **Terry Pratchett**, *Small Gods* | Theology as mechanism — the joke that a god's power is an accounting problem |
| Anachronism as texture | **Catherynne M. Valente** | Modern furniture inside myth, delivered flat and at speed |

## Traps

**Madeline Miller** is the name everyone reaches for, and would be the wrong model.
*Circe* and *The Song of Achilles* are lyrical, interior and elegiac — she would write a
devastating Riddy and a beautiful Caeneus and have nothing to do with Zeus's voicemail
spree. The same caution applies to most of the current myth-retelling shelf, including
**Jennifer Saint**: the register is reverent, and KAOS is reverent about nothing,
including grief.

**Do not import a warm humanist ending.** Pratchett is on the list for the theology, not
the consolation. This story burns a kitten to make a point about love and expects the
reader to sit in it.

**Do not let Prometheus narrate the whole book in one key.** His authority is borrowed
and the ending depends on that being felt before it is stated. If the voice is equally
confident on the last page as on the first, the reveal is dead on arrival — the prose has
to thin, hesitate and start guessing across the final act.

**Do not explain the political system.** No character in Season 1 ever describes the
extraction economy to another character except Prometheus, to the reader, once. Everyone
else simply lives and dies inside it.

---

## Recommended approach

Read **North** for how a divine narrator holds and loses a story, **Haynes** for the tonal
mix, and keep **Abercrombie** open beside the draft for any scene in which someone dies.
Use **Barker** before writing Hecuba or Andromache, and **Peters** before writing Caeneus.

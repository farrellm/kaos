# Characters

Dossiers for every named figure in Season 1. Each entry: **role**, **wound**,
**arc**, **exit position**, and **voice**. Cross-references:
[episodes](03-EPISODES.md) · [mysteries](05-MYSTERIES.md) ·
[cosmology](02-COSMOLOGY.md).

---

## THE NARRATOR

### Prometheus
**Role.** Titan, prisoner, narrator. Chained to a rock on a plateau within sight of
Zeus's villa; an eagle eats his liver daily and it grows back. He speaks directly to
the audience throughout, and is the only character who does.

**Wound.** He loves Zeus. That is the tragedy he names himself in Episode 8: he had
the chance to destroy Zeus and could not do it, and *that weakness* is the only
reason Zeus still rules. He also murdered his lover Charon on instruction, to make
his plan possible, and has spent thousands of years chained within conversational
distance of the man he betrayed everyone for.

**What he claims.** That the myth is wrong — he wasn't punished for giving humans
fire, he was punished for telling Zeus he'd become everything he hated. That Zeus
was born mortal, did not create humanity, and therefore can be destroyed by
humanity. That prophecies are given only to humans. That his plan needs three
humans in the right place at the right time, and this is the only chance.

**Arc.** Lies to Zeus's face in Episode 1 to keep his confidence ("prophecies are for
humans"; "the Meander doesn't blip"), while telling the audience the opposite.
Steers Zeus away from wiping out half the world and toward stories. In Episode 6,
deliberately plants the idea of Minos defying his prophecy — which detonates Minos,
Krete, and Zeus's grip in one move. In Episode 8, his reading of the Fates' promise
fails in front of him: Caeneus enters the water and no freedom comes, and he despairs
on the page. He vanishes mid-sentence during his last speech to Zeus, and the Fates
free him only after the renewal — telling him his freedom is his own problem now.

**Exit position.** Free, uncaged, standing inside Zeus's house, telling Zeus that
his vision was real and that kaos is coming. Visibly less in control than he has
spent eight episodes implying.

**Voice.** Wry, second-person, avuncular; the register of a man telling you a story
he has told himself many times. Drops into raw grief with no transition. Comfortable
with dramatic irony and openly enjoys withholding.

## THE GODS

### Zeus
**Role.** King of the gods. Absolute power, terrible taste, no self-knowledge.

**Wound.** Kronos. His father tormented him; he killed him; he became him. Everyone
who loves him tells him so — Prometheus, Hades, Hera — and he cannot hear it. He is
also, underneath everything, a man whose children do not visit.

**Arc.** Finds a wrinkle on Olympia Day and reads it as the prophecy's first line.
Escalates continuously: skeet-shooting his own ball boys over a missing watch;
"phase one" natural disasters; "phase two," engineered hatred; eavesdropping on his
wife through the tacitas' severed tongues; striking Hades with lightning; burning
the Fates alive; rationing the Meander to his own family; incinerating Hera's bees;
killing Dionysus's kitten to prove that love is weakness; seizing every city
including the City of the Dead.

**The boy he was.** Prometheus keeps two pieces of evidence: the ants Zeus burned as a
child after one bit him, and wept over for a week; and **Hestia**, the puppy Kronos ran
over with a cart, whom Zeus loved back to life and who stayed with him ever after.

**Key belief.** That he is order itself — that he took a world of chaos and made it
make sense — and therefore that fate is unnecessary.

**Exit position.** Alone, having converted every relative into an enemy, unable to
find anyone in his own house. He has had a vision of himself bleeding, which is not
supposed to be possible.

**Voice.** Mid-century American showbiz cadence. Self-interrupting. Grandiose nouns
crashing into crude ones. Nicknames, endearments, and abrupt violence in the same
breath. Talks about himself in the third person when frightened.

### Hera
**Role.** Queen of the gods. Zeus's wife and sister. Runs the tacita network and,
through it, the confession economy of the entire mortal world.

**Wound.** She is queen of a house she is not permitted to leave, married to a man
whose infidelities she is expected to manage while her own is a capital matter. She
has no children of her own on screen; her power is built on other people's secrets.

**Arc.** Enforces the "no more kids" agreement by turning Zeus's pregnant lovers into
bees and killing the newborns. Conducts a long-running affair with Poseidon.
Talks Zeus down off a ladder in Episode 5 with a masterpiece of gaslighting after he
hears her voice in a confession booth. Threatens Minos into filicide by appealing to
him as a parent. Refuses Poseidon's offer to run away, because the house is hers.

**Exit position.** Ordered by Zeus to stay put while he takes everything. Immediately
phones someone — never identified on screen — and tells them to gather the troops
and make up a spare bed. See [05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md).

**Voice.** Cut glass. Endearments as weapons. Utterly unbothered by cruelty,
including her own; the coldest and most fluent speaker in the series.

### Poseidon
**Role.** God of the sea. Owns Krete through President Minos. Hedonist, coward,
company man.

**Wound.** He is in love with Hera and always has been ("forever"), and will never
have her.

**Arc.** Dismisses Zeus's paranoia, then obeys him anyway. Drowns a servant for
sport. Waterboards Minos in his own shower to remind him who owns him. Goes to the
Fates to confirm Minos can kill his son — and is told a true thing that everybody
misunderstands. Admits his love to Dionysus in exchange for silence.

**Exit position.** Stripped of Krete and every other city, told to grow up, and in
love with a woman now under house arrest.

**Voice.** Lazy, tactile, sensual, faintly stoned. Comic until abruptly not.

### Hades
**Role.** God of death. Runs the underworld. Built the Frame on Zeus's instruction.

**Wound.** He did what he was told, and the thing he built is a soul-shredder. His
own recorded orientation video promises the newly dead that everything is going to
be okay. Prometheus names him precisely: he did what Zeus told him to, and that is
his tragedy.

**Arc.** Discovers a soul has escaped the Nothing; conceals it from Zeus; performs a
manual extraction on the boy Nax while Riddy and Caeneus watch. Realizes the Nothing
is structurally overloaded and that the whole cosmos could break. Confronts Zeus
with the truth and a metaphor involving juice, and is struck by lightning into a
swimming pool. Refuses to release Orpheus on principle — the dead stay dead — then
is overruled by his wife. Resolves to go back into the Nothing and find a way to
restore souls.

**Exit position.** Standing in front of Caeneus, having just watched a dead human do
what no god can: renew a soul.

**Voice.** Soft, apologetic, middle-management. Says "I love you" to his brother as a
preamble to a difficult conversation. Genuinely kind and genuinely complicit.

### Persephone
**Role.** Queen of the underworld. Runs it in practice while Hades falters.

**Wound.** The myth. Every child on Earth is taught she was abducted and raped, and
pities her; she loves her husband and is furious that the lie was manufactured. She
is allergic to pomegranate.

**Arc.** Argues for standards when Zeus demands more divers immediately. Pushes Hades
to tell Zeus the truth. After Zeus maims him, refuses to be summoned, then comes
anyway — with a sandwich. Countermands Hades's ruling and sends Orpheus back to Earth
with Riddy, explicitly as a statement of intent against Zeus: *the war has begun.*

**Exit position.** In the underworld, the most decisive rebel among the gods, having
already committed the first act of war.

**Voice.** Dry, practical, protective. Delivers policy and affection in the same tone.

### Dionysus
**Role.** God of pleasure, madness and wild frenzy. Zeus's son by a mortal woman whom
Hera turned into a bee. Made a full god by his father, which nobody has forgiven.

**Wound.** He is not taken seriously by anyone, including himself, and he wants to be
loved more than he wants to be powerful. He has never been in love and says so.

**Arc.** Wants a promotion — god of love, war, wisdom, anything with weight. Steals
Zeus's watch to be noticed. Finds Orpheus with a gun to his head and adopts him as a
purpose. Bribes the Fates with the watch to get a failed contestant into the
underworld. Watches Orpheus's love up close and converts from stunt to belief.
Blackmails Poseidon into silence, then keeps the secret anyway. Confesses everything
to his father in Episode 8 and is answered with a dead kitten and a lecture on love
as weakness.

**Exit position.** On Earth with the Trojans, offering to do whatever they want. What
they want is to rebuild Troy and destroy Olympus. **Nax is not there** — he was killed on
Olympia night and extracted from the Nothing by Hades in Episode 5, and the name does not
occur anywhere in Episodes 6–8. The Episode 8 scene carries no speaker tags at all, so that
the survivors present are Andromache and Hecuba is **(inference)**.

**Voice.** Warm, profane, puppyish, easily wounded. The only god who likes humans and
says so.

### The absent Olympians
Named on Zeus's answering-machine spree in Episode 2 and never seen: **Ares**,
**Athena**, **Apollo**, **Aphrodite**, **Artemis**, **Hermes**. None picks up. Zeus's
theory is that he keeps making more children on Earth because the existing ones
disappoint him. **Hercules** is referenced as a friend who gave Zeus the lucky watch
that saw him through his twelve labors. **Perseus** is a party invitation, possibly
with a winged horse.

## THE THREE HUMANS

### Eurydice ("Riddy")
**Role.** First of Prometheus's three. Married to Orpheus, the most famous musician
in Krete. Mother became a tacita when she was five.

**Wound.** She has been a wife and a muse and nothing of her own. She has no purpose
and knows it. Her mother chose Hera over her and cut out her tongue for the honor.

**Arc.** Wakes intending to leave Orpheus and cannot. Is told by a stranger
(Cassandra) that today's the day, that she leaves everyone today, and that her love
is dead. Confesses to her tongueless mother in a booth, declares she defies the gods,
walks into traffic and dies. Arrives in the underworld to find she was buried without
a coin — Orpheus took it, so she would wait for him. Trapped for two hundred years.
Meets Caeneus. Discovers the Nothing. Works out with him that Renewal is a lie and
that "the family" in their shared prophecy is the gods. Sent back to Earth by
Persephone, over her own objection, to tell the living.

**Exit position.** Alive on Earth, having released Orpheus, met Cassandra, and been
told that she is now a prophet and must go to Ari.

**Voice.** Deadpan, self-deprecating, quick to anger, allergic to sentiment.

### Caeneus
**Role.** Second of the three. Amazon; trans man; ten years dead; underworld dog
handler promoted to diver.

**Wound.** Everything and everyone. His people executed him for existing. His mother
led them to him and then took his coin, stranding him among the unresolved for a
projected two hundred years. He has spent ten years living entirely in the past,
waiting at the port for her to arrive.

**Arc.** Promoted to diver by Medusa on Persephone's orders — not a coincidence.
Befriends Riddy. Follows Nax into the Nothing; when Hades extracts the boy by hand,
Riddy is left reeling and Caeneus feels nothing at all — an immunity he cannot explain,
and which Prometheus flags to the audience: *he will soon.*
Discovers his prophecy is word-for-word identical to hers, which
should be impossible. Deduces with her that there is no Renewal. Sends her back to
Earth to tell the living, and stays to stop the Frame from below. Is refused help by
Medusa, who tells him there is a plan beyond him. His mother finally arrives dead by
her own hand, having waited ten years to keep an appointment with his prophecy, and
walks into the river. He goes in after her.

**Exit position.** In the Lethe, holding his mother — and she is *back*. He has renewed
a human soul, in front of Hades, and neither of them knows how.

**Voice.** Gentle, funny, patient. The kindest person in the series. Wry about his own
suffering in a way that keeps other people from having to sit in it.

### Ariadne ("Ari")
**Role.** Third of the three. Daughter of President Minos of Krete. Believes she
smothered her twin brother Glaucus in her sleep as an infant.

**Wound.** A murder she did not commit, thirty years of penance for it, and a mother
who has never let it go — an annual waxwork of the brother, sculpted by a captive
genius, whose nose is never right.

**Arc.** Unveils the desecrated monument. Is taken to the Munis by her bodyguard
Theseus, who is a Trojan operative; sees the Furies in the crowd. Trades her birthday
wish to pardon the Trojan 7 — and her father releases them, then feeds them to the
Minotaur anyway. Is abducted by the Furies and shown film of her father ordering
Daedalus to kill the firstborn twin. Confronts Daedalus, who confesses: Glaucus never
died. He is the Minotaur. She goes into the labyrinth, reaches her brother, unmasks
him — and then her father arrives to kill him.

**Exit position.** She killed Minos. She was the first to draw breath; she came out
screaming; the prophecy was always about her. Krete is hers by default and she has
announced she will not rule for the gods. Her mother has been shown the truth and
approves of the killing.

**Voice.** Clipped, sardonic, formal in public and foul-mouthed in private. Rage held
just under the surface at all times — her mother says she came into the world and
filled it with rage, and it is meant as an accusation.

## KRETE

### Minos
**Role.** President of Krete. Poseidon's appointee and property.

**Prophecy.** *"Your end begins in the marital bed. The first child to draw breath
will kill you dead."*

**Wound.** He believed he had beaten it. He had twins, ordered the firstborn killed,
raised the survivor as his heir, and told her the death was her fault — thirty years
of borrowed guilt to cover his own.

**Arc.** Imprisoned Daedalus so his talents could not be sold elsewhere and so his
crime stayed local. Kept his surviving son in a dungeon, then discovered the boy could
kill, and had him weaponized into the Minotaur. Pardons the Trojan 7 for his
daughter's birthday and executes them the same night. Is ordered by Hera and Poseidon
to kill his son and does it, on live broadcast to Olympus. Then, in the flush of
having defied fate, tells Ari the truth about her birth.

**Exit position.** Dead, killed by Ari with a knife. The prophecy was correct; the
reading was wrong.

**Voice.** Statesmanlike in public, wheedling before gods, tender and manipulative
with his daughter. Loves her, genuinely, and has ruined her.

### Glaucus / the Minotaur
**Role.** Minos's firstborn son. Born quiet, born blue, born first. Kept in the dark
from infancy, forbidden a name, fed by guards he learned to bite.

**Arc.** He killed a jailer and his father was impressed — that was the moment he
became a weapon rather than a secret. Daedalus built his mask and horns, cared for
him, and made a small bell so the boy would know it was him coming and be gentle. The
button that opens the cage is the sound that turns him into a beast.

**Exit position.** Dead. Ari reaches him, says his name, calls herself his twin and
takes off the mask — and Minos arrives and knifes him where he stands (E7). In E8 she
brings her mother down to the body: *Mama, I tried to save him. I really tried.* He
died having had almost no language. Ari says *twin* at him and the word comes back
**(inference — the transcripts carry no speaker tags, and the soft repetition reads as
his rather than hers)**.

**Voice.** Growls, roars, fragments. When he speaks, it lands like a hammer.

### Ari's mother (Pasiphaë, unnamed in dialogue)
Grief made permanent. Commissions the annual waxwork, criticizes its nose, and tells
her daughter every year that Glaucus was perfect and Ari was rage. Never saw the body
— only a coffin. At the end she is brought to the throne room, shown that her son is
alive, told her husband did it for the gods, and told her daughter killed him. Her
response is one word: *good.*

### Daedalus
**Role.** Palace artificer. Prisoner in all but name for roughly thirty years.

**Wound.** He killed a baby because Minos threatened his son Icarus, and Icarus died
anyway — building wings to escape a father he had learned was a murderer.

**Arc.** Sculpts Ari's waxworks. Sets the table for two out of habit. Confesses
everything when Ari finally asks, having sworn to himself he would tell the truth if
she or her mother ever did. Takes her into the labyrinth. Mauled by Glaucus when
Minos opens the cage remotely.

**Exit position.** Last seen attacked in the labyrinth; Ari sends staff to fetch him
in Episode 8. His survival is not confirmed on screen — see
[05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md).

**Voice.** Mild, self-effacing, wrecked. Speaks about his own atrocity in the flat
tone of a man who has rehearsed the confession for decades.

### Theseus
Ari's bodyguard; secretly working with the Trojans. Takes her to the Munis for her
birthday because it is a place for rage. Leads her to Nax and asks her to save a life.
Apologizes for lying to her; she does not forgive him quickly. Not seen after Episode 3.

## TROY

### Nax (Astyanax)
Andromache's son, thrown from the walls of Troy as a child and presumed dead for
years. Secret prince, seventh Trojan, and the man who put the shit on the monument.
Wants to turn himself in; his friends lock him up to stop him. Pardoned by Minos,
then killed with the other six that same night. In the underworld he ends up inside
the Nothing, incompletely processed, and Hades extracts him personally while Riddy and
Caeneus watch. Funny, brave, and self-deprecating right up to the point where he is
begging for help.

### Andromache
Nax's mother. Diplomat by necessity. Manages Minos with careful deference while her
mother-in-law does not. Watched her husband butchered and her child thrown from the
walls, got him back, and lost him again the same night.

### Hecuba
Former queen of Troy. Refuses the deference entirely; asks Minos whether an adopted
child should not have equal rights, and does not soften it. Prometheus's line about
both women: they were very tired.

**Exit position (both).** Alive, with Dionysus offering them anything. What they ask
for is Troy rebuilt and Olympus destroyed.

### Cassandra
Prophet. Cursed to be disbelieved. Appears in Episode 1 as a shoplifter arrested for
cat food, ranting that the gods are stealing our souls and that she told them about
the horse and the men inside it. Tells Riddy that today's the day, that she will leave
everyone, that her love is dead. Reappears at the exit of the Cave in Episode 8 to tell
Riddy she is a prophet now, that she must go to Ari and set the living free while
Caeneus does the same for the dead. Everything she says is true. Nobody has ever
listened. Her presence at both ends of Riddy's arc is unexplained — see
[05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md).

## THE UNDERWORLD

### Medusa
Runs the divers. Snakes for hair; the stone-gaze does nothing to the dead, since they
are already dead. Selects Caeneus specifically and will not say why. Reveals to Riddy
that there are rebels — people who see the gods for what they are — and asks if she
wants to help save the world from Zeus. Later shuts Caeneus down hard: he doesn't
understand the whole picture, Riddy was the important one, there is a plan beyond him,
keep his head down. Brisk, funny, entirely unsentimental.

### Charon
Ferryman of the Styx. Prometheus's lover, murdered by him in the Episode 4 flashback
after being made to swear he would one day help a stranger identifiable by a mark.
He keeps the promise: ferries Orpheus, taunts him and Anatole into a lethal choice,
then saves Orpheus from Charybdis when he sees the mark. Bitter, funny, cruel on
purpose, and still doing what he swore.

### Polyphemus ("Poly")
Keeper of the Cave, the quiz show through which the desperate attempt to buy passage
into the underworld. Master of ceremonies; takes the entry fee; is casually
brutalized by the Fates. Runs a scam that turns out not to be entirely a scam.

### Prue
Underworld handler, promoted alongside Caeneus. Applied for the diver job many times
and was rejected; is thrilled. Chatty, lonely, faintly ridiculous, kind. Her prophecy
was about a dark-haired creature and a winter fall, and she has made it fit her dead
pony and her insomnia. Fond of Caeneus.

### Adrian
One of the unresolved. Explains the etiquette of the dead: they eat, sleep, shower and
salt their food to feel normal, and the ones who ask questions get into trouble.
Cried at a party once.

### Agatha
Consenting human sacrifice from the Olympia broadcast; dies on camera in Episode 3 and
is on the ferry in Episode 2. Lived to die, as the saying goes, and at the water's edge
finds herself thinking of everything she didn't do. Pious, snobbish, and — at the last
moment — afraid.

### Carl Crixus
Spartan gladiator killed at the Munis on Ari's birthday, and part of the pair who arrive
in the underworld with Hippolyta still spoiling for a fight. His death is the one Ari
watches, and the reason she says she can't remember why she thought a Munis would cheer
her up.

### Fotis
Caeneus's cerberus dog. The name means "light." Left behind when Caeneus is promoted;
pisses on a fax machine; loose in Episode 4 in the sequence that leads Caeneus to Nax.

### Caeneus's mother
Amazon. Named him — told him his male name would be Caeneus — and told him she loved
him, in the same conversation in which she told him to leave. Then led the hunters to
him. Shot herself the night he was buried without a coin, then waited ten years,
because the day had to be the right day. Walks into the Lethe in front of him to prove
the Frame is not a lie. She is the soul Caeneus renews.

## FORCES OLDER THAN THE GODS

### The Fates — Clotho, Lachesis, Atropos
Write every prophecy. Set the questions and keep the score at the Cave. Bribable, at
least by a god with a good enough watch. Speak in overlapping triplets, bicker about
staging, and enjoy themselves enormously. To Zeus in Episode 8: they are everywhere,
inside him and outside him, they are him and they are nothing to do with him; the
possibility of a prophecy exists but without belief it cannot come to pass. They let
him burn them, choosing the poolroom because they like it. They are alive
afterward — fate cannot be destroyed — and they free Prometheus, telling him that all
he had to do was trust and wait.

### The Furies — Alecto, Tisiphone, Megaera
Older than the gods. Roam the Earth delivering justice where they judge it denied,
which is subjective. Only their target can see or hear them; they drive people to
suicide. A favor stands between them and the Fates, and it is spent on Ari — abducting
her, feeding her popcorn, and showing her the film of her father's crime, on the explicit
grounds that they are not there to punish her but to show her the truth. Who owed whom is
never stated: a Fate (the subtitles tag Atropos) is present when the debt is closed —
*we're even now, no more favors, she's on her own* — and the likelier reading is that the
Furies were repaying the Fates, since showing Ari the truth services the Fates' design
**(inference)**. Their advice to a devout woman: fuck the gods; they are the source of
all your pain. Then they let her walk out.

## AND

### Dennis
Riddy and Orpheus's kitten. Passed to Dionysus for feeding when Orpheus goes into the
underworld, and carried around thereafter in a sling. Zeus kills him in front of
Dionysus in Episode 8 to demonstrate that a god does not love anything lesser than
himself. It does not demonstrate that. It is the moment Dionysus leaves.

### Orpheus
**Role.** Musician. Riddy's husband. Not one of the three, and therefore, in
Prometheus's cold assessment, useful: an ego that accepts a cosmic purpose without
suspecting the purpose might be co-opted.

**Wound.** He loves his wife the way a spotlight loves a stage, and cannot tell the
difference between devotion and consumption. Then he takes her coin — so she cannot
move on without him — and spends the rest of the season learning what he is.

**Arc.** Attempts suicide; is shot in the shoulder by Dionysus instead. Fails the
Cave's quiz when the Fates expose the coin, and is smuggled in anyway on the price of
Zeus's watch. Crosses the Styx, murders his fellow contestant Anatole under Charon's
goading, is saved from Charybdis by the mark on his hand. Survives the wilderness by
refusing the Fates' offered water, choosing to die trying rather than live having
quit. Reaches Riddy — who will not come back. Is refused by Hades and then released
anyway by Persephone. Learns Riddy was leaving him the day she died.

**Exit position.** Alive, alone, on Earth, having been thanked and left. He is the only
human alive who has walked into death and out again, and no one has any use for him
yet.

**Voice.** Effusive, needy, generous, theatrical. Says "I love you" as punctuation.
Capable of enormous grace and casual monstrousness within the same hour.

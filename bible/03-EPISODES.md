# Episode Breakdown

Beat-by-beat for all eight episodes. Each entry: **logline**, **beats**,
**Prometheus's narration** (what he tells the audience directly), **first
appearances**, and **what this plants** — cross-referenced to
[05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md).

The season covers only a handful of days on Earth; see [04-TIMELINE.md](04-TIMELINE.md).

---

## Episode 1

**Logline.** On Olympia Day, Zeus finds a wrinkle, a monument is desecrated, and
Eurydice dies on the day she finally meant to leave her husband.

**Beats**
1. Prometheus introduces himself as a prisoner who knows too much, and introduces
   Zeus — a transcendent, unmitigated bastard — and the plan to bring him down: three
   humans and a prophecy.
2. Krete. Riddy shops for cereal she can't remember her husband asking for. A woman is
   dragged out for stealing cat food, ranting that everyone steals and the gods steal
   souls; she tells Riddy that today's the day, that she will leave everyone today, and
   that her love is dead. (This is Cassandra; she is not named until Episode 8.)
3. Olympus. Zeus performs the weather, then checks the Meander and thinks he sees a
   blip. Hera assures him it looks normal.
4. Dionysus buys a watch on a stranger's advice and gives it to his father, who
   already has one — a lucky talisman from Hercules — and rejects it. Dionysus asks for
   a promotion and is called lightweight by Hera. He is thrown through a table for
   telling her to suck a dick.
5. The Olympia broadcast: a consenting human sacrifice, then the unveiling of the
   monument, which has been smeared with excrement and the words *fuck the gods*.
6. Zeus wants to wipe out half the world. Hera talks him down: power is delegation;
   call Poseidon.
7. Riddy and Orpheus at home. He has written a song named after her; she asks whether
   he could love her quietly. She cannot say the thing she means to say.
8. Zeus finds the wrinkle. Prometheus is delivered by eagle to reassure him: prophecies
   are for humans, the Meander doesn't blip, you cannot be destroyed. The prophecy is
   read aloud for the first time.
9. Prometheus, alone with the audience: he is lying. Zeus can absolutely be destroyed.
10. Dionysus on the streets of Krete, insulted, told by a fan-turned-stranger to find a
    purpose for himself, not his father. He passes Ari in the crowd — *remember her.*
11. Riddy goes to the tacita booth. It is her mother. She confesses that she no longer
    loves Orpheus, that she prayed for ten years for her mother to come home, that she
    knows her prophecy and doesn't want it. She declares she defies the gods. Hera,
    listening, tells her to shut up.
12. Orpheus dedicates the new song to her from the stage. Riddy walks into the street
    and is hit by a truck. Cassandra, at the scene: I told you you'd leave him today.
13. Zeus checks the mirror — the wrinkle is there, then isn't, then is.
14. The funeral. Orpheus cannot place the coin and someone else does it for him.
15. Orpheus buys a gun and goes to the beach. Dionysus tackles him and is shot in the
    shoulder. He does not bleed. *What if I told you you could see your wife again
    without dying?*
16. Prometheus's closing address: only humans receive prophecies; Zeus was once one of
    you; this is our only chance.

**Prometheus's narration.** The plan and its three humans. Riddy's ignorance of her own
significance. The tacitas' confessions feed Hera. Dionysus's mother was mortal and Hera
turned her into a bee. The cruelty of the gap between perception and reality. That
Orpheus will do anything to get Riddy back — anything.

**First appearances.** Prometheus, Zeus, Hera, Dionysus, Riddy, Orpheus, Cassandra
(unnamed), Ari (in passing), Minos (on TV), Calli, Dennis, Agatha.

**What this plants.** The wrinkle and the blip. The shared prophecy. Cassandra's
foreknowledge. The watch, twice — Hercules's and Dionysus's. Riddy's mother the tacita.
The Trojan graffiti. Dionysus's purpose. Orpheus's willingness to do anything.

## Episode 2

**Logline.** Riddy arrives in an underworld that has no place for her; Orpheus is taken
to a game show; Zeus's children don't answer the phone.

**Beats**
1. Cold open, ten years earlier: a young bartender is recognized by Amazon women as
   *Caenis*, told he has dishonored the gods and his tribe, and shot dead with a
   crossbow. His mother is present — he says *Mom?* as he dies — but that she gave the
   order is **(inference)**; Episode 4 shows Hippolyta firing the shot.
2. Orpheus wakes hungover with Dionysus in his kitchen. They must move before Riddy
   goes through the Frame.
3. The ferry across the Styx, and Hades's orientation video: death is the only
   certainty, your devotion is about to pay off, everything's going to be okay.
4. Zeus on speakerphone with Hades and Persephone, demanding more divers immediately to
   punish the monument shitters. Persephone argues for standards; Hades falls asleep.
   Zeus asks whether either of them has seen his watch, and is hung up on.
5. Riddy meets Agatha on the ferry — a sacrifice expecting a step up.
6. Dionysus drives Orpheus at lethal speed to prove he can't die; a test of faith.
7. The port. Cerberus dogs single Riddy out. Caeneus, a handler, is promoted to diver by
   Medusa alongside Prue, over his objections: he is waiting for his mother. Medusa
   selected him. He hands over Fotis.
8. Prometheus introduces Caeneus as the second human — dead ten years, ignorant of his
   significance, living entirely in the past.
9. The Cave. Orpheus refuses to be humiliated; Dionysus insists his love is greater than
   death. Entry fee paid with a wedding ring. The Fates preside.
10. Zeus works through his children's answering machines — Ares, Athena, Apollo,
    Aphrodite, Artemis, Hermes. Nobody is home.
11. Zeus with a pregnant lover; Hera arrives wearing another woman's face, extracts the
    baby, turns the mother into a bee, and orders Zeus to kill the newborn. He does.
12. Riddy and the other coinless are told the truth: no coin, no Frame, no Renewal.
    Two hundred years. She insists there has been a mistake. There are no mistakes here.
13. Quiz rounds: freckles and blemishes; a father tells his son's story in mime; Orpheus
    must sing his love without his voice, and plays it.
14. Riddy tries to enter the water at the Lethe. It will not rise to meet her. Caeneus
    finds her weeping and tells her he is the same — buried without a coin, and it gets
    better.
15. The Fates expose the coin in Orpheus's pocket. Game over.
16. Dionysus buys Orpheus's passage anyway, with Zeus's watch. Prometheus: never trust a
    myth.

**Prometheus's narration.** Caeneus's death and stasis. The coinless and the water that
won't rise. That Riddy is exactly where she needs to be and has met exactly who she
needs to meet.

**First appearances.** Caeneus, Medusa, Prue, Charon (voice at port), Polyphemus, the
Fates, Hades, Persephone, Fotis, Anatole and his parents.

**What this plants.** Caeneus's mother and the crossbow. The missing watch as a
season-long fuse. The two-hundred-year sentence. Hera's bees and the murdered
half-siblings. Persephone's professional pride. The Cave as a real door.

## Episode 3

**Logline.** Ari's birthday, told from the beginning: a waxwork, a monument, a Munis,
and a promise her father breaks the same night.

**Beats**
1. **Alecto** and **Tisiphone** drive a man named Terry to shoot himself in a gas-station
   bathroom, then pick their next target.
2. Prometheus introduces the third human. Ari's twin Glaucus died when she rolled onto
   him as an infant; her mother never accepted it, so in many ways he never went away.
3. The annual waxwork sitting with Daedalus. The nose is wrong, as always. Minos offers
   Ari any birthday present she wants; she declines, as always.
4. The Olympia ceremony: Agatha's sacrifice, the doctrine of Renewal, then the
   monument — and the excrement.
5. Minos interrogates Hecuba and Andromache. Six of the Trojan 7 are held in the
   labyrinth. If the seventh presents himself, the six will be pardoned; otherwise, the
   Minotaur. Deadline midnight.
6. Poseidon and Zeus spar over the blasphemy. Zeus orders Krete brought to heel; it's a
   command, not a request. Poseidon drowns a servant for fun, then phones Hera, who tells
   him to just do what Zeus asks.
7. Poseidon visits the temple in disguise, confirms Minos will use the Minotaur, and
   calls the monument a piece of crap that looks better with the shit on it.
8. Ari argues for mercy toward the Trojans. Minos refuses: the gods choose who rules.
   She asks whether he wishes she were Glaucus; he says Glaucus gave her his place.
9. Theseus takes Ari to the Munis — a place for pain and rage — for her birthday. She
   sees the Furies in the crowd. A fighter dies.
10. Ari tells Theseus the Furies are coming for her because she killed her twin. He asks:
    if you could save a life, would you?
11. He takes her to the seventh Trojan — Nax, who is Astyanax, thought dead since Troy.
    He wants to surrender; his people locked him up to stop him.
12. Ari asks her father for the Trojans as her birthday present. He grants it. The seven
    walk free.
13. Poseidon waterboards Minos in his shower over the pardon: there is no decision if I
    tell you to do something; you don't bend to love.
14. Poseidon and Hera in bed. She would be quite keen on a prophecy that got rid of Zeus
    alone.
15. Midnight. The Trojans are rounded up again. The Minotaur is released. Ari runs to
    Andromache's screams and is told: *you did this.*
16. Minos: it's the will of the gods — or would you have me defy them?
17. The Furies arrive for Ari. *We're here about your brother.*

**Prometheus's narration.** What the Furies are. Ari's accidental murder. Troy's fall and
what was done to Hecuba and Andromache. That love can overpower reason. That this is what
the gods always do: where there is connection they divide, where there is love they
destroy it.

**First appearances.** Ari, Minos, Daedalus, Theseus, Ari's mother, Hecuba, Andromache,
Nax, Poseidon, the Furies, the Minotaur (unseen, growling).

**What this plants.** Minos's obedience and its price. Daedalus never leaving the palace.
The button that opens the Minotaur's cage. Ari's guilt as inherited fiction. Hera's
appetite for a Zeus-only prophecy. The Furies' interest in Ari.

## Episode 4

**Logline.** Prometheus's oldest crime is revealed; Orpheus crosses the Styx and murders
for it; Riddy and Caeneus find the Nothing.

**Beats**
1. Zeus interrogates and shoots ball boys over the missing watch. Hera phones Poseidon:
   a war room, two phases, a serious escalation — try the boy.
2. Flashback: Prometheus and Charon, lovers. Prometheus makes Charon swear to protect a
   stranger he will know by a mark. Then he strangles him. *Try not to hate me.*
   Prometheus to the audience: the greater good always comes at a personal cost.
3. Orpheus and Anatole enter the underworld through a bin. Poly warns them. Orpheus asks
   Dionysus to feed Dennis. The Fates hand out ill-fitting clothes: you get what you get
   and you do what you're told.
4. Charon meets them, knows Orpheus took his wife's coin, and agrees to a wary truce.
5. Prometheus on Orpheus: an ego like that has no trouble accepting a cosmic purpose, and
   no suspicion the purpose might be co-opted.
6. Persephone tries to leave Zeus a message that a soul has escaped the Nothing; Hades
   deletes it. He will fix it himself, and there is absolutely no need to tell Zeus.
7. Caeneus, walking Fotis, finds Nax outside the Nothing — terrified, saying he's been
   through the Frame already and they have to help *all of them, in there.*
8. Asphodel's canteen: Adrian explains the etiquette of the dead. Salt so you can taste
   it. Don't ask questions; you won't get answers.
9. Dionysus is summoned by Poseidon, who warns him: if a mortal brings someone back from
   the dead, Zeus will destroy you and try to destroy us all. Get the watch back.
10. Hades leaves Zeus a cheerful voicemail about excellent new divers and no problems
    whatsoever.
11. At the Lethe, Riddy meets Agatha again and snaps at her; Caeneus talks a stranger
    through the Frame.
12. Charon stops mid-river: he can only take one. He lied. He'll wait for one of them to
    break the pact. Orpheus kills Anatole.
13. Hippolyta, the Amazon who killed Caeneus, arrives dead and recognizes him. She tells
    him his mother gave him up freely. Very freely.
14. Dionysus tortures a shopkeeper for a look-alike watch, then passes the replacement
    off to Zeus as the original, claiming he found it by the gate. (The real watch went
    to the Fates in Episode 2 — Zeus wears a counterfeit from here to the end.)
15. The unresolved hold a party. Prue's prophecy game. Riddy refuses to share hers.
    Riddy and Caeneus talk on the steps: the Amazons, his transition, being murdered by
    his own people, what kissing feels like.
16. Charybdis takes Orpheus; Charon sees the mark and saves him. *It means something is
    true.*
17. Zeus thanks Dionysus for the watch, promises him power, and lays out phase two:
    hatred, seeded small — one rape, one dead child — until continents burn. You may play
    with humans, but never forget you are not one of them.
18. Medusa phones Hades: they've found the soul.
19. Riddy and Caeneus follow Nax's screams into the Nothing and find a woman they had helped
    through the Frame that morning, standing among the ruined — *"We just sent her through
    the Frame."* The line is untagged in the transcript, so which of them says it is
    **(unestablished)**; Riddy was coinless and never a diver, so the *we* is complicity
    rather than job description.

**Prometheus's narration.** His love for Charon and the cost of the greater good.
Orpheus's usefulness. That others require much more help.

**First appearances.** Charon (in person), Anatole, Adrian, Hippolyta, Carl Crixus,
Charybdis, the Nothing.

**What this plants.** The oath and the mark. Hades's cover-up. Orpheus as a murderer.
Hippolyta's claim about Caeneus's mother. The Nothing itself. Zeus's phase two.

## Episode 5

**Logline.** The Furies show Ari the film of her own life; Zeus hears his wife's voice in
a confession booth; Hades harvests a boy in front of two witnesses.

**Beats**
1. Prometheus corrects the myth: he was punished for standing up to Zeus, not for fire. A
   visitor — unseen and unidentified — comes to his door before his imprisonment and
   tells him it is not his fate to kill Zeus; he must kill Charon, regain Zeus's trust,
   and submit to his punishment, because there is a greater plan.
2. The Furies bring Ari to a private cinema with popcorn and show her the truth.
3. Film: Poseidon installs Minos. Three rules — this is all on loan, I put you here, and
   if you please me I can make you immortal. Task one: release a lion into the city.
   Task two: produce an heir. Minos objects that his prophecy forbids children and
   recites it. Poseidon's solution: have twins, kill the one that comes out first.
4. Film: the twins as infants, sleeping curled together. Minos orders Daedalus, his
   prisoner, to kill the firstborn. Daedalus takes the baby away.
5. Ari refuses to watch the end and demands to be let out. She accuses the Furies of
   driving humans mad until they kill themselves. **Megaera**'s answer, seconded by
   **Alecto** and **Tisiphone**: fuck the gods —
   they're the source of all your pain. They let her go. A favor between the Furies and
   the Fates is settled — a Fate (tagged Atropos) is present, and who owed whom is never
   stated **(inference: the Furies read as the repayers)**; she's on her own. Parting
   advice: talk to Daedalus.
6. Ari returns home. Minos is frantic with relief. She asks him to recite his prophecy;
   he detonates, then apologizes.
7. Daedalus confesses: he killed the baby on Minos's orders because Minos threatened
   Icarus — and Icarus died anyway, building wings to escape after he learned what his
   father had done. Then the correction: Glaucus is not dead. He is the Minotaur.
8. Hera and Poseidon in bed. She keeps the tacitas because their tongues keep her
   secrets — well, her secret.
9. Zeus and Dionysus execute phase one: earthquakes, floods, fire. Zeus feels vital.
10. Poseidon warns Dionysus to keep an eye on his mortal, and mocks Zeus about Krete.
    Zeus announces they're bombing Krete right after he takes a piss.
11. Zeus enters Hera's forbidden bathroom, listens to the tongues, and hears his wife's
    voice in a stranger's confession.
12. Dionysus visits his mother's hive: he isn't enjoying this anymore and can't talk to
    his father. Zeus, elsewhere, tears the voice box out of a tacita who won't write.
13. Hera confronts him. The oath. Zeus accuses her of the prophecy's third line. Hera's
    counter-speech: she eats his pain, she could have taken the Earth or the underworld,
    she is queen and that was the oath they swore. She dares him to produce the evidence.
    He can't find it. She talks him down off the ladder and he weeps in her arms.
14. Prometheus: all actions have consequences, even for Zeus. Hera orders the tacita
    buried.
15. Riddy and Caeneus in the Nothing. Hades finds Nax, tells him there's no need to be
    afraid, and extracts him by hand. Riddy is knocked down by the pain; Caeneus feels
    nothing at all and doesn't know why. Prometheus: *he will soon.*
16. Hades recites the prophecy to himself: *what have I done?* Prometheus: he did what
    Zeus told him to; that is his tragedy; Caeneus and Riddy must undo it.
17. Caeneus offers to sit with Riddy. She declines. Prometheus: love is the point of
    everything — and often the problem.

**Prometheus's narration.** The real reason for his punishment. The visitor and the
greater plan. That if one of them fails, they all fail. Hades's tragedy and the two
humans who must undo it.

**First appearances.** Megaera by name, Poseidon's installation of Minos (flashback),
young Glaucus, the mechanics of manual extraction.

**What this plants.** The unidentified visitor — the largest unanswered question in the
series. Minos's prophecy in full. Glaucus alive. Hera's singular secret. Zeus's oath-
breaking. Riddy's sensitivity to soul-extraction, and Caeneus's inexplicable immunity to it.

## Episode 6

**Logline.** Prometheus tells the audience what the Frame is really for; Hades tells Zeus
the system is breaking and is struck by lightning for it.

**Beats**
1. Caeneus tells Riddy how he left the Amazons: his friend Leos came back and was killed;
   Sofie, the woman who had harbored him, is left wailing and reminded that she knew the
   rules; his mother named him Caeneus and told him to go; her own prophecy had foretold a
   boy.
   They agree they hate the gods. They kiss.
2. Charon leaves Orpheus at the wilderness — the final test, which nobody has ever reached.
3. Persephone dresses Hades for the confrontation. The Nothing is overloaded; that's why
   the Frame glitched; the whole place could break, and not just down here.
4. Prometheus talks Zeus out of destroying the Fates by pointing out that the prophecy has
   no meaning except the one he insists on giving it — and mentions, apparently in
   passing, that President Minos lives in direct defiance of his. Zeus seizes on it: make
   Minos kill his son.
5. Riddy and Caeneus compare prophecies and find they are identical. Impossible.
6. Hades confronts the family: the prophecy is real and Zeus is making it come true. A
   soul escaped, unfinished, requiring manual extraction. The Frame must be reset to a
   baseline level of Renewal. Zeus forces him into the pool, accuses him of wanting to
   rule, calls him the runt, and blasts him with lightning.
7. Riddy and Caeneus reason it out: there is no Renewal; everyone who goes through the
   Frame ends up in the Nothing; the family that falls is the gods.
8. Persephone, over Hades's body, vows to find a way to destroy Zeus.
9. Poseidon consults the Fates. They confirm Minos can kill his son, recite Minos's
   prophecy, and confirm that people can occasionally defy their prophecies.
10. **Prometheus's central monologue**: Zeus killed Kronos and absorbed his soul; the
    power was intoxicating; he learned to repeat it; eventually the Frame was built to
    harvest and liquidize souls at industrial scale. The gods drink them. The lie must be
    preserved and the humans distracted.
11. Hera insists on going to Minos with Poseidon: he needs to succeed. She threatens
    Minos with Ari's suffering if he fails. He will kill his son.
12. The wilderness. Orpheus, dying of thirst, is offered water by Clotho — with the small
    print that drinking means losing his wife. He refuses and chooses to die trying. That
    was the test. He passes.
13. Orpheus reaches Asphodel and finds Prue, who knows Riddy.
14. Dionysus discovers Hera and Poseidon together on a boat.
15. Charon takes a phone call: the man with the mark is here. *Someone's finally going
    back. Maybe.*

**Prometheus's narration.** The origin of the gods and the true function of the Frame —
the season's thesis statement.

**First appearances.** Leos (flashback), young Caeneus, Clotho in the wilderness.

**What this plants.** The identical prophecies. Persephone's vow. Hera and Poseidon
exposed to Dionysus. Prometheus's fingerprints on the Minos plan — he supplied the idea
that kills Minos, topples Krete, and destroys Zeus's composure.

## Episode 7

**Logline.** Two households collapse at once: Hades refuses Orpheus, and Minos kills his
son on live broadcast before his daughter kills him.

**Beats**
1. Dionysus and Poseidon trade blackmail and end up trading confidences instead.
   Poseidon has loved Hera forever. Dionysus wants that, whatever it is.
2. Orpheus and Riddy finally meet, in front of Caeneus and Medusa. He apologizes for the
   coin; he has come to take her back to life. She refuses — she has things to do here,
   a job, friends.
3. Medusa pulls Riddy aside: rebels; people who see the gods for what they are; do you
   want to help save the world from Zeus?
4. Olympus. Zeus's barbecue, a television, and no Hades. Zeus explains they are going to
   watch a human defy his prophecy.
5. Daedalus tells Ari how her brother became the Minotaur: the dark, the namelessness,
   the biting, the first killing, and her father's approval. Daedalus made the mask and
   the horns — and also a bell, so the boy would know it was him and be gentle.
6. Caeneus persuades Riddy to go back: tell them what we saw; tell them to live, for
   themselves, not the gods; tell them all the best things are human.
7. Hades receives Orpheus with real warmth — no human has ever come this far — then
   refuses him. The final test was convincing Hades, and he hasn't. Orpheus offers to
   swap places. Denied. He may stay, dead, with his wife.
8. In the lift, Persephone tells Hades he fumbled a chance to make a statement Zeus
   couldn't ignore. Hades: that's an act of war. Persephone: the war has begun. He
   insists the dead stay dead and that the answer is in the Nothing — he will find a way
   to restore souls. She sends Medusa to fetch Orpheus's wife anyway.
9. Daedalus takes Ari into the labyrinth. Minos discovers Daedalus is missing and cannot
   be found. He reaches the control room and pushes the button; Glaucus mauls Daedalus.
10. Ari faces her brother, says his name, calls herself his twin, and takes off the mask.
11. Minos arrives. Ari refuses to move. She names his crime, calls him the monster, and
    tells him to shut his mouth about the gods. Minos kills Glaucus with a knife.
12. Olympus erupts in celebration. Zeus is euphoric and hungry.
13. Minos, elated, tells Ari the truth: he defied his prophecy; the gods are on his side;
    her brother was weak, she came out screaming, and the burden of blame made her great.
14. Ari realizes she was the first to draw breath. She kills him.
15. Zeus collapses. Dionysus, watching Ari, says he thinks he's in love.

**Prometheus's narration.** None of substance — the episode runs almost entirely without
him, which is itself a signal that events have outpaced his commentary.

**First appearances.** Glaucus unmasked and speaking.

**What this plants.** Persephone's unilateral act of war. Medusa's rebels. Hades's plan to
restore souls. Ari as ruler by patricide. Dionysus's declaration.

## Episode 8

**Logline.** Zeus burns the Fates and rations his family; Caeneus goes into the river after
his mother and brings her back; Prometheus walks free and finds he was never the author.

**Beats**
1. Zeus dreams he is bleeding and a Fate tells him gods don't bleed. He wakes convinced it
   was a vision, not a dream, and goes to destroy the Fates.
2. Hera summons the family. Persephone refuses to be summoned, then comes with a sandwich.
3. Hades tells Medusa he has taken too much from humans, that he has a plan to restore
   things, and that he is going to defy his brother.
4. Zeus and the Fates. They know why he's come and help him stage it. They explain that a
   prophecy requires his belief to come to pass, that they are everywhere and nowhere,
   and that he should perhaps be scared of those moving against him. **Atropos** wants somewhere less predictable
   than the stage; they settle on the poolroom. He burns them. As Lachesis dies he sees Dionysus's watch on her wrist.
5. Persephone tells the assembled gods why the story about her abduction enrages her: she
   loves her husband, she was never kidnapped, she is allergic to pomegranate, and every
   child on Earth pities her for a lie.
6. Medusa refuses to help Caeneus stop the Frame: he doesn't understand the whole picture,
   Riddy was the important one, there is a plan beyond him, keep his head down.
7. Riddy and Orpheus climb out to Earth. She tells him she was leaving him the day she
   died; that it had become need, not love. He asks her to begin again; she says she has
   to go back — and then, at the surface, she says goodbye instead. He drives away alone.
8. Caeneus finds Prue and Adrian at the river and begs them to help him stop people going
   through the Frame — and his mother is standing there.
9. She shot herself the night he was buried, then waited ten years, because it had to be
   today: today she takes him through the Frame. She walks into the water to prove it.
   He goes in after her.
10. Prometheus, watching, panics: the Fates promised Caeneus would be protected, that when
    he went through the Frame *the family falls* would happen, and that Prometheus would
    be free.
11. Olympus. Zeus produces rationed bottles of Meander water — one each, none for Hera —
    announces he destroyed the Fates, and declares that there is no more fate, only
    loyalty. Dionysus confesses everything about the watch and Orpheus, and explains that
    he did it because humans get lied to and deserve a few more years. Zeus praises him,
    then kills Dennis to prove love is weakness, then sets Hera's bees on fire.
12. Zeus strips Poseidon of every city including the City of the Dead, tells Dionysus to
    grow up, tells Persephone that Hades can collect his own bottle, and confines Hera.
13. Zeus visits Prometheus wanting congratulations for defying his prophecy. Prometheus
    tells him the truth: he had his chance and couldn't do it, and *that* weakness is the
    only reason Zeus still rules. Mid-sentence, Prometheus vanishes. The Fates — alive —
    free him, telling him fate can't be destroyed and all he had to do was trust and wait.
14. Ari brings her mother to Glaucus's body — he was alive all thirty of those years, and
    her husband did it — and tells her she killed their father. Her mother
    says: good. Ari announces she will not rule for the gods.
15. Riddy meets Cassandra, who names herself, tells Riddy she is a prophet now, and gives
    her the charge: go to Ari and set the living free; Caeneus will do the same for the
    dead.
16. In the river, Caeneus's mother speaks — *kaos* — and something passes between them.
    Hades, arriving: *you can renew human souls.*
17. Hera phones an unnamed party: gather the troops and make up a spare bed.
18. Prometheus appears inside Zeus's house and tells him the vision was real, and kaos is
    coming.
19. Dionysus asks the Trojans what they want. Rebuild Troy and destroy Olympus.

**Prometheus's narration.** Sparse and, for the first time, panicked. He does not know what
is happening. His last line to Zeus is a warning, not a plan.

**First appearances.** Cassandra by name. Caeneus's mother in the present.

**What this plants.** Everything in [05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md), and specifically:
Caeneus's power, Hera's phone call, Cassandra's charge, the surviving Fates, Prometheus's
loose freedom, Dionysus's alliance, and Zeus alone in a house he has emptied.

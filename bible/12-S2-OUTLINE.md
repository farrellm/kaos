# Season 2 — Chapter Outline

**Status: invention, not evidence.** Like [11-S2-CANON.md](11-S2-CANON.md), this is
constructed for the novelization and is not derived from the show. It assumes the three
decisions in that file (D1, D2, D3).

Forty chapters in three parts. The brief is that Season 2 **closes** — every thread left
open by Season 1 resolves here, and the ledger at the end proves it.

---

## The structural decision

**Prometheus narrates until he cannot, and then Riddy does.**

[D1](11-S2-CANON.md) makes his authority a thing the book spends and withdraws. So: he
narrates Part One in full command, degrades across Part Two as he learns what he was, and
loses the narration outright in Chapter 24 — mid-scene, mid-paragraph. Part Three is
narrated by Riddy.

The reason it must be Riddy: Cassandra told her she is a prophet now, and a prophet in this
world is someone who tells a true story that nobody believes. When she takes the narration,
**the reader becomes the first person who has ever believed one of them.** That is the
payoff of Cassandra's curse, and it is only available to a novel — no other form can hand
the reader that job.

Prometheus does not get the last word. He gets a final chapter in which he appears in the
third person, described by someone else. He loses even the first person.

*This is the one call to redirect if you disagree; the beats below survive a different
narrator, the chapter assignments don't.*

---

## The engine

Divinity is a supply chain. The Nothing is the reservoir. Caeneus can run it backwards.

So the war is not fought over Olympus — it is fought over the inventory. The resistance
intends to empty the Nothing and return its souls to the world. Zeus intends to keep the
reservoir full by any means, which means going back to the method he used on Kronos.

**Cost, established early and paid throughout:** every soul Caeneus renews takes a piece of
his own past. The man who spent ten years living entirely in what had already happened pays
in what has already happened. He forgets Leos first. He forgets his mother in the middle.
By the end he does not know Riddy.

# PART ONE — THE SPARE BED
*Chapters 1–13. Narrated by Prometheus, in full command. Days, not weeks, after the Season 1
curtain.*

**1.** Olympus, minutes later. Prometheus, free for the first time in millennia, discovers
that Zeus is not afraid of him — he pours him a drink and asks him to sit. Freedom turns out
not to be power. *(O3)*

**2.** Hera arrives at a walled compound in the north with two bags and no retinue. Her son
opens the door. **Ares** has had the troops for centuries and has simply never been asked.
*(O9, O15)*

**3.** Heraklion, celebrating a dead president badly. Riddy walks into a city that has no
idea what to do with itself.

**4.** The Lethe. Caeneus's mother is breathing. Hades — god of death, on his knees on a
riverbank — asks a dead man how he did it, and gets no answer, because Caeneus doesn't know.

**5.** Orpheus drives until the fuel runs out. He goes back to the Cave and finds it shut,
Poly gone, the bin welded. The one door he knows is closed.

**6.** Ari has Glaucus moved to a room with a window. He has been alive thirty years and has
never seen weather. *(O12)*

**7.** Zeus, rationing. He is the only one drinking freely and it is not enough. The wrinkle
has company now. *(P2)*

**8.** Dionysus, with the Trojans, learns Nax is dead — has been dead since the night of the
pardon — and that he agreed to help avenge a boy he never knew was gone. *(O11)*

**9.** Riddy reaches Ari and is disbelieved until she recites Ari's own prophecy back to her.
Two of the three humans meet on Earth for the first time.

**10.** Hades and Persephone disagree for the first time in the series: protect Caeneus, or
use him. *(P3)*

**11.** Poseidon arrives at Ares's gate, cityless, and asks for the spare bed. It is already
taken. *(O10)*

**12.** Asphodel. Medusa recruits among the unresolved — Prue, Adrian, and several thousand
people serving a two-hundred-year sentence for a Renewal that never existed. *(O14)*

**13.** Prometheus goes to the Styx to see Charon, who will not speak to him. He tells
himself it is grief.

# PART TWO — THE RESERVOIR
*Chapters 14–27. Prometheus narrating, with the confidence coming off him a layer at a time.*

**14.** The plan gets its name: empty the Nothing. Persephone says it out loud and Hades
does not object, which is how everyone knows it is real.

**15.** The first mass renewal. It works. Eleven souls go back to the world.

**16.** Caeneus cannot remember Leos's face. The cost is identified and Riddy is the one who
identifies it, because she is the only person who has heard him tell the story twice.

**17.** Daedalus is found alive in the labyrinth, maimed, having survived on what Glaucus
left him. *(P5)*

**18.** Ari commissions him to build the opposite of his life's work: not a maze, a door.
Something that lets one man's capacity serve a reservoir. He says it will take longer than
they have.

**19.** Orpheus starts singing the truth in public. He is arrested in three cities and
released in all three, because arresting him makes it worse. His fame — the flaw that made
him unbearable — turns out to be the only distribution network the resistance has. *(O8)*

**20.** Zeus notices the Meander running thin. He goes back to the old method, personally,
on a minor god who will not be missed. It works. He is calm for the first time in the book.
*(P2, O5)*

**21.** Theseus returns to Ari's service and brings the Trojans with him. Krete and the
Trojan remnant ally — the first alliance in the story made by humans, without a god
brokering it. *(O12, O16)*

**22.** Cassandra takes Riddy to meet the network: eleven people in a room above a
fishmonger's, holding prophecies between them going back four hundred years. *(D2, newQ2)*

**23.** And the problem with the network: prophecies are a stock, not a supply. Zeus abolished
fate, the Fates are content to let him believe it, and what these eleven people hold is
everything anyone will ever have. *(newQ3, newQ6)*

**24.** Charon tells Prometheus what he was. Not out of cruelty — because he has known since
before he died and has been waiting to be asked. **The narration breaks here**, mid-account,
and does not recover its footing. *(newQ1, D1)*

**25.** Prometheus goes to the Fates for a correction and receives none, because they have
never said an untrue thing to him and are not about to start. *(O4, partial)*

**26.** Hera, Ares and Poseidon strike Olympus and fail. Zeus kills one of his own children
in front of the others to end the argument. The absent Olympians stop being absent. *(O15)*

**27.** Zeus puts the Nothing under direct guard and cuts Caeneus off from the river. The
reservoir is now a garrison.

# PART THREE — WHAT THE HUMANS DID
*Chapters 28–40. Narrated by Riddy.*

**28.** Riddy takes the narration and says so. She is a prophet; nobody has ever believed
one; she is going to tell it anyway. *(O7)*

**29.** The unresolved rise. They are the only population in the underworld with a sentence
they now know is fictional, and there are more of them than there are guards. *(O14)*

**30.** Daedalus's door opens early, badly, and holds. The Nothing begins to drain.

**31.** Caeneus at scale. He works for eleven days and comes out not knowing his mother's
name. Riddy holds his past for him and reads it back nightly. *(newQ4)*

**32.** Zeus ages in public. The Meander fails in the middle of a sentence. His children
arrive — all six — not to fight, just to watch. *(P2, O15)*

**33.** Hera takes Olympus and finds it worthless: the throne was a distribution point and
there is nothing left to distribute. She sits in it anyway, because it is hers. *(O9)*

**34.** Poseidon dies covering her retreat from a war she has already won, and she is
genuinely, inconveniently grieved. *(O10)*

**35.** Prometheus and Zeus, last conversation. He still cannot do it. This time he
understands that not doing it was never the failure — it was the reason he was chosen.
*(O3)*

**36.** Caeneus renews Zeus. Not kills — **renews**: takes a soul assembled out of stolen
human futures and returns it to the world as one mortal life, owed nothing. The god who
absorbed his father is handed back the thing he sold. *(P2, O5)*

**37.** The Fates, working again. What they wanted was never Zeus's death — it was a world
in which a thread can be written truthfully, which is impossible while one being is
hoarding the futures the thread is made of. Prophecy resumes. *(O4, newQ3)*

**38.** Kaos, defined. Not disorder — the end of management. People die and go back and
nobody administers it. The fourth clause was never a catastrophe; it was a description of
ordinary life, which the gods had been calling chaos for four thousand years. *(P1)*

**39.** Endings, quickly, the way the show does them. Krete votes and Ari loses the first
one. Glaucus learns eleven words. Dionysus stays mortal-adjacent and does not get Ari.
Hades and Persephone reopen the port with no ferry fare. Medusa runs it. Fotis is old.
Orpheus sings the last true song and is believed, which is all he ever actually wanted and
he does not enjoy it. *(O8, O11, O12, O13, P3, P4, O16)*

**40.** Riddy and Caeneus. He does not know her. She tells him who he was — the dog, the
balcony herbs, the boy who came back for him, the mother who waited ten years — and he
listens the way you listen to a story about someone else. Then he asks her name, and she
tells him, and that is the first thing he learns. Prometheus appears in this chapter in the
third person, walking on a beach, described by someone who is not him.

## Deliberately withheld

Things the outline refuses to resolve, on purpose:

- **Dennis is not renewed.** The kitten stays dead. Dionysus's whole arc is learning that
  loving something that can be taken is not weakness; undoing the loss would retroactively
  make Zeus right.
- **Nobody thanks Caeneus.** He pays the entire cost and ends without the memory of having
  paid it. No character delivers a speech about his sacrifice, because he would not be able
  to follow it.
- **Ari loses the first vote.** She spends the season refusing to rule for the gods and then
  discovers that ruling for people is a separate skill she does not yet have.
- **Orpheus and Riddy do not reconcile.** He gets belief, which is what he wanted. He does
  not get her, which is what he thought he wanted.

## Craft reminders while drafting

From [09-VOICE-AND-STYLE.md](09-VOICE-AND-STYLE.md) and
[10-INFLUENCES.md](10-INFLUENCES.md), the four that will be hardest to hold across forty
chapters:

1. Never let a character explain the extraction economy to another character. Riddy may
   explain it to the reader. That is the narrator's privilege and nobody else's.
2. Kill people mid-sentence and move on. Grief arrives three chapters later, displaced.
3. Keep the comedy in the mouths of the powerful. It is how power sounds from inside.
4. The Fates never lie. Every line they speak must be technically true.

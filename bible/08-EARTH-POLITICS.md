# Earth — Krete, Troy, the Amazons, Athens

The mortal world as a political system: who rules, who is ruled, and what religion is
actually for. See [01-CHARACTERS.md](01-CHARACTERS.md) for the people and
[03-EPISODES.md](03-EPISODES.md) for the sequence of events.

## KRETE

A modern city-state — cars, sirens, television news, supermarkets, a police force, a
presidential palace in Heraklion. Ruled by **President Minos**, who is not elected in any
sense that matters.

### The presidency as divine property
Poseidon installed Minos personally after the previous president proved headstrong and
ended up dead. The terms, delivered at the handover: everything is on loan; I put you
here, you serve me; please me and I can make you immortal.

Minos's own summary of political theory, to Ari: the gods choose who rules, and people
can never be trusted to vote for what they need. When Ari points out that Athens votes and
it works, and that the people would elect him anyway because they love her father, he
declines the argument entirely.

**Enforcement** is physical. When Minos pardons the Trojans as a gift to his daughter,
Poseidon holds him under the water in his own shower and explains that there is no
decision if a god tells him to do something, and that you do not bend to love.

### State religion
- **Olympia Day** — the annual holy day. A public ceremony with a choir, a broadcast, a
  doctrine reading, an **annual human sacrifice**, and the unveiling of a monument.
- **The sacrifice** is consenting and framed as an honor: the volunteer dies in the name
  of Olympus, secure that her piety assures the most glorious Renewal. Agatha of Heraklion
  is that year's.
- **The doctrine.** Humans are born by the grace of Zeus; the best live in service to the
  gods; obedience begets a new and better life. *There is no death, only Renewal.* The
  response to everything is **"Vero"** — the affirmation, roughly "true," used as amen.
- **The tacita booths.** Confession, publicly framed as comfort. Actually Hera's
  intelligence network. See [06-OLYMPUS.md](06-OLYMPUS.md).
- **Coin bearers and funerals.** The rite invokes Hades directly.
- **Blasphemy is a criminal offense.** Cassandra is charged with it in Episode 1 for
  saying the gods steal souls.

### The Munis
Gladiatorial combat to the death, publicly attended, with an announcer and a sales pitch:
the loser will be buried with honor and renewed with splendor. Theseus's defense of it is
the show's sharpest line about the function of state spectacle — it is a place for pain,
for rage, a place where people can let it go.

### The labyrinth
Prison and weapon in one. Political prisoners are held in cells beneath the palace. At its
center is **the Minotaur** — Glaucus — released by a button that turns a light on his cage
from green to red. No survivors have been recorded. Execution by Minotaur is the state's
capital punishment and its deterrent.

**Daedalus** is the palace artificer and a prisoner in all but name for thirty years,
permitted "a lot of range within the grounds." Minos's stated reason, as Daedalus
understands it, was to stop him working elsewhere; the real reason was that he knew.

### After Minos
Ari kills him in Episode 7. By Episode 8 she holds the palace, has revealed the truth to
her mother, and has stated she will not rule for the gods — while Zeus has simultaneously
announced he is taking personal charge of every city. See
[05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md) O12.

## TROY

### The fall
Troy was destroyed. Both Hecuba's and Andromache's husbands were butchered in front of
them. Andromache's only child, Astyanax, was thrown from the city walls at the surrender.
Cassandra warned about the horse and the men inside it and was not believed.

Minos's official position is that Troy was destroyed for a reason: they got complacent,
they overreached, and if the gods choose to punish a people it does well not to question
the choice. Poseidon's private position is that the Trojans are already screwed hard
enough — too easy now.

Prometheus supplies the real mechanism in Episode 2, in Zeus's own voice: the war was
manufactured. Stories seeded to make people hate each other, one man tempted to steal
another's wife, a child burned for a favorable wind, and then ten years of conflict the
participants believed they wanted.

### The Trojans in Krete
Refugees under segregation. No equal rights, no proper schools, no integration — the
official justification being that segregation is for their own safety. Population: more
than five million before the fall, roughly **fifteen thousand** left.

Two strategies in tension:
- **Andromache** works within the system. She is deferential to Minos, calls the monument
  desecration a prank by young and stupid people, and swears to see them punished — buying
  time and lives.
- **Hecuba**, former queen, refuses the register entirely and asks Minos to his face
  whether an adopted child shouldn't have equal rights, proper schools, integration.

Prometheus's verdict on both: they were very tired.

### The Trojan 7
Young Trojans who smeared the Olympia monument with excrement and the words *fuck the
gods*. Six captured; the seventh — Nax, whose idea it was — hidden by his own people
against his will. Minos's offer: the seventh presents himself and the six are pardoned.
Ari spends her birthday wish instead, and all seven walk free. That night they are
rounded up again and fed to the Minotaur.

### After
Hecuba and Andromache are alive; Nax is dead; Dionysus has offered them anything they
want. What they want is Troy rebuilt and Olympus destroyed.

## THE AMAZONS

A closed compound society, studied in Krete's schools. Rules, as Caeneus describes them:

- Boys must leave the compound when they come of age — around eleven for most, though
  Caeneus stayed until fifteen.
- Men who return are **executed**, and so are the women who harbor them. Caeneus's friend
  Leos came back and was killed; the woman who sheltered him, Sofie, was told that she knew
  the rules.
- The gods are honored; deviation from tribal order is framed as dishonoring both the gods
  and the tribe. Hippolyta calls Caeneus a blasphemer and offers his exclusion from Renewal
  as proof.
- Nothing grows there — Caeneus's memory of the compound is that nothing did, which is why
  the balcony herb pots in his Krete apartment matter so much to him.

**Caeneus's mother** knew what he was before he said it — the form doesn't fit the content
— gave him his name, sent him to a friend in Krete, and told him she loved him. Ten years
later she led the hunters to him and gave him up. Hippolyta's version, offered as cruelty:
she gave him up freely, very freely. Her own version, offered as love: he had to fulfill
his prophecy, and today's the day.

**Hippolyta the Second** is an Amazon champion who kills Caeneus with a crossbow, then
dies in the Munis and arrives in the underworld cheerfully unrepentant.

## ATHENS

Named twice and never seen. The Athenians vote, and Ari says it works. Athens is the
show's one gesture at an alternative political arrangement on Earth — a live option for a
ruler of Krete who has broken with the gods, and a piece of unused ground.

## What religion is for, structurally

Zeus states the theory himself in Episode 3, arguing with Poseidon: humans must be kept on
their toes, because if they are relaxed and enjoy peace too long they are less
constructively scared, and if they are less scared they have less desire to honor the
gods — and blasphemy without consequence is genuinely dangerous.

The entire apparatus — the sacrifices, the festivals, the confession booths, the coins, the
monuments, the doctrine of Renewal — exists to keep human souls flowing voluntarily into a
machine that destroys them. Fear supplies the compliance; the promise of a better next life
supplies the consent.

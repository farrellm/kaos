# The Underworld — The Bureaucracy of Death

The underworld is a workplace. It has an orientation video, a staff canteen, uniforms,
shift rosters, a promotions process, a PA system, a fax machine, and a management chain
that reports upward to a man who does not read the reports.

See [02-COSMOLOGY.md](02-COSMOLOGY.md) for the metaphysics of what the machinery is
actually doing.

---

## The journey, step by step

**1. Death and the coin.** Every mortal has a **coin bearer** responsible for burying or
cremating them with a coin. The funeral rite asks Hades to accept the coin and grant safe
passage through the Frame, blessing the transition and the Renewal of all human souls.

**2. The ferry across the Styx.** A passenger vessel with a PA system. Passengers are
asked to assemble on the bridge deck for an orientation video presented by Hades himself:
death is the only certainty, some of you will be scared or angry, your devotion to the
gods is about to pay off, the better you served the more glorious your next life,
everything's going to be okay. He built the Frame, he says, to allow your atoms to return
joyfully to the Earth. *Do not mourn life. You are returning to it.*

**3. The port.** Arrivals form five lines through turnstiles. **Handlers** work with
**cerberus dogs** — working animals, not to be petted — which detect the coinless and
single them out. Those flagged are pulled aside "for a bit of admin."

**4. The bad news.** The coinless are taken to a briefing room and told: you were buried
or cremated without a coin, you cannot pass through the Frame, do not feel shame, take your
time. There is no appeal, because *there are no mistakes here.* They are told they will
wait **two hundred years** for another chance, and to praise Hades for his generosity.

**5. Asphodel.** Where the unresolved live. Living quarters, a canteen, a games room, a
bar, basketball courts, allocated work sectors, flyers for social events. The dead eat,
drink, smoke and shower — none of which they can taste or feel — because it is routine and
routine makes them feel normal. Beer tastes of nothing. Salt is used so food tastes of
almost something.

**6. The River Lethe.** Reached by subway. Where souls actually enter the water and pass
through the Frame. Announcements ask arrivals to queue, to treat divers with respect, and
warn that aggression toward divers will not be tolerated.

**7. The Frame.** The gate. Souls pass through it into what everyone believes is Renewal.

**8. The Nothing.** Where they actually go.

## The staff

**Divers.** Guide, support and persuade the dead into the water and through the Frame.
The job exists for one reason: **nobody can be forced.** Free will is inefficient — which,
as Medusa says during training, is exactly where divers come in. Traditionally an
internal promotion requiring years of service and an application; Zeus's demand for
immediate expansion in Episode 2 overrides both, which is how Caeneus is conscripted
without ever having applied.

Failure penalty, per Medusa: you'll be working Children's Renewal.

**Handlers.** Work the port and the dogs. Charon runs them. Lower status than divers, and
Charon resents having staff poached.

**Medusa.** Manages the divers. Reports to Persephone. Selects Caeneus deliberately and
will not explain why. Later reveals the existence of rebels and recruits Riddy.

**Charon.** Ferryman of the Styx and floor supervisor at the port. Has a day off, a fax
machine, and a very short temper. Also the keeper of a thousands-year-old oath.

**The Fates and Polyphemus** sit on the Earth side of the border, running the Cave.

## Chain of command

```
ZEUS  — sets policy by phone, never visits, deletes nuance
  |
HADES — god of death, built the Frame, avoids the Nothing
  |
PERSEPHONE — runs the place in practice; issues the diver orders
  |
MEDUSA — divers                CHARON — ferry, port, handlers
  |                              |
DIVERS (Caeneus, Prue)        HANDLERS, dog teams
  |
THE DEAD
```

Zeus's actual management technique in Episode 2: demand more divers today, dismiss
Persephone's professional objections as crowd control rather than art, and hang up. Hades
sleeps through the call.

## The Nothing

A vast, dim, echoing space full of standing figures — ruined, silent, still recognizably
people. Riddy and Caeneus reach it in Episode 4 by following Nax's screams, and recognize
a woman they had personally sent through the Frame that same morning.

**What is known.** It is where processed souls end up. It has been neglected by Hades
personally for a very long time. It is over capacity, and that overcapacity is what made
the Frame glitch and release Nax "unfinished." Hades performed a **manual extraction** on
Nax by touch — an old-school method, and the first escape in history.

**The stakes as Hades states them.** There are too many souls. Nothing goes back to the
Earth anymore. The souls in there are not given the time or space to disintegrate. It will
eventually break — and not just the underworld: Olympus and Earth too.

**Zeus's ruling.** The Frame stays as it is. You deal with the Nothing. That's your job.

## The Cave — the door from the other side

An Earth-side venue entered through a bin, run by Polyphemus, judged by the Fates.
Publicly considered a scam; nobody has ever brought anyone back. Contestants pay an entry
fee, compete before a live audience, and the winner is granted entry to the underworld to
attempt a retrieval.

Rounds seen in Episode 2: mark every freckle, mole and blemish on your loved one's body;
tell us about your son without speaking; sing of your love without your voice. The Fates
set the questions and keep the score "on several levels."

Winning is only the entry. Below there are further tests:
1. **Crossing the Styx** — Charon's rule that he takes only one, designed to make
   competitors kill each other.
2. **Charybdis**, in the water.
3. **The wilderness** — a desert nobody had ever reached. The test is thirst: a Fate
   offers water with the condition that drinking forfeits your wife. Orpheus refuses and
   chooses to die trying.
4. **Hades himself** — the final test is convincing him, and he is under no obligation.

## The rebellion inside the machine

- **Persephone** ordered Caeneus's promotion, argued for standards against Zeus's
  efficiency demands, and unilaterally released Orpheus and Riddy as a deliberate act of
  war after Hades refused.
- **Medusa** knows about the Nothing, calls the rebels by name, recruits Riddy, and shuts
  Caeneus down with the assertion that there is a plan beyond him.
- **Charon** has been waiting thousands of years to honor a promise made to the man who
  murdered him, and takes a phone call confirming that the man with the mark has arrived.
- **Hades** is not a rebel until Episode 8, when he tells Medusa he has taken too much
  from humans and is going to defy his brother.
- **Caeneus and Riddy** are the floor-level insurgency: stop people going through the
  Frame, and tell the living the truth.

By the final episode the underworld contains an organized resistance at every level of its
own hierarchy, and the one person who could report it upward — Zeus — has just placed
himself in direct charge of the department.

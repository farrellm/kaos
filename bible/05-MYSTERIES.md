# Mysteries — Resolved, Partially Resolved, Open

The load-bearing file. Every question Season 1 raises, with the evidence, a clear
separation of what is **confirmed on screen** from what is **inferred**, and —
for unresolved items — a set of **resolution constraints**: what any Season 2
answer must satisfy to remain consistent with the show.

These are constraints, not proposals. **No answers are chosen in this file** — the
constraints below are the audit trail, and they stay exactly as first written even where
a decision has since been taken against them.

Where one has, the entry carries a pointer to the decision in
[11-S2-CANON.md](11-S2-CANON.md) and to the chapters it runs in
(**bold** = drafted, plain = outlined only). Everything in this file was written from the
transcripts; everything it points at is invention.

# PART I — RESOLVED

Questions the season poses and then answers outright. Listed because a novelization
must not accidentally reopen them.

### R1. Who desecrated the monument?
The Trojan 7, and the idea was Nax's. He says so directly in Episode 3 and repeats it,
weeping, outside the Nothing in Episode 4: it was his fault. Six were captured; he was
the seventh and was locked up by his own people to stop him surrendering.

### R2. Why can't Riddy pass through the Frame?
Orpheus took her coin from the funeral so she would be forced to wait for him. Exposed
by the Fates at the Cave in Episode 2, admitted by Orpheus repeatedly thereafter.

### R3. What is the Frame really for?
Harvesting. Prometheus's Episode 6 monologue: Zeus absorbed Kronos's soul at the moment
of killing him, learned to repeat it, then industrialized the process. The Frame
liquidizes human souls; the gods drink them to stay immortal; Renewal is the cover story.
Confirmed independently from below by Riddy and Caeneus, who deduce the same thing from
the Nothing.

### R4. Did Ari kill her brother?
No. Minos ordered Daedalus to kill the firstborn twin on Poseidon's instruction, Daedalus
did not go through with it, and Glaucus was raised in the dark as the Minotaur. Ari has
carried a fabricated guilt for thirty years. Established by the Furies' film (E5),
confirmed by Daedalus (E5, E7).

### R5. What was Minos's prophecy, and did he defy it?
*"Your end begins in the marital bed. The first child to draw breath will kill you dead."*
He did not defy it. He misread it: Ari, not Glaucus, was the first to draw breath —
Glaucus was born first but born blue and silent, and Ari came out screaming. Minos
murdered the wrong child and was killed by the right one. The Fates never lied to
Poseidon; they told him Minos *could* kill his son, which was true.

### R6. Why did Prometheus murder Charon?
Because a visitor told him to, as the price of a greater plan (E5). Charon's oath — to
protect a stranger identifiable by a mark — is the mechanism that keeps Orpheus alive in
Episode 4. **The visitor's identity remains open — see O2.**

### R7. Who took Zeus's watch, and where did it end up?
Dionysus took it to get his father's attention, then paid it to the Fates as the price of
Orpheus's entry into the underworld. The watch he "finds" and returns in Episode 4 is a
shop-bought look-alike, so Zeus wears a counterfeit for the rest of the season — which is
why seeing the real one on Lachesis's wrist as she burns detonates the way it does.
Dionysus confesses in full in Episode 8.

### R8. Is Persephone in the underworld against her will?
No, and the myth that says so was manufactured. She loves Hades, was never abducted, and
is allergic to pomegranate. Her Episode 8 speech is unambiguous, and it establishes that
the gods author human belief deliberately.

### R9. Why was Caeneus selected as a diver?
Persephone ordered it ("Persephone's orders, Charon," E2), and Medusa later admits there
is a plan beyond him and that Riddy was the important one (E8). Medusa's training-day
line — "I chose you specifically for that reason" — is, in context, about guiding people
who can't swim, and is weak evidence of plan-selection on its own. **What the plan
required of him remains open — see O6.**

# PART II — PARTIALLY RESOLVED

Answered in part, with a load-bearing piece missing.

### P1. What does the shared prophecy actually mean?
> **Settled — [DC29](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **38**. Not disorder — order that nobody owns.
**Known.** *"A line appears, the order wanes, the family falls, and kaos reigns"* is held
by Zeus, Riddy, Caeneus and Ari. Riddy and Caeneus establish in Episode 6 that "the
family" is the gods. The first three clauses have visible referents by the end of the
season: Zeus's wrinkle; the graffiti and the collapse of obedience; the fracturing of
Olympus.

**Missing.** What "kaos reigns" is — a state, a person, an outcome — and whether it is
what Prometheus wanted. Caeneus's mother says the word aloud in the river as the soul
returns, which suggests the fourth clause begins there **(inference)**.

**Constraints.** (a) The prophecy is shared by four beings, three of them the humans
Prometheus selected, one of them their target. Any reading must account for Zeus holding
it too, despite prophecies supposedly being human-only. (b) Ari has fulfilled hers in one
sense already — she killed the man who ruled — yet remains a live participant. (c) The
duplication is explicitly flagged as impossible by the characters, so it must be
meaningful, not a coincidence.

### P2. Can Zeus be killed, and is he already mortal?
> **Settled — [DC15, DC21](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **7**, **20**, **32**, **36**.
**Known.** Prometheus states Zeus was born mortal and can be destroyed by humans. Zeus's
divinity is sustained by Meander water. He has found a wrinkle, seen a blip in the
Meander, and had a vision of himself bleeding while a Fate tells him gods don't bleed.

**Missing.** Whether the wrinkle is aging, whether the vision is a prophecy or a warning,
and what would actually kill him.

**Constraints.** Zeus's immortality is a supply chain: it depends on harvested souls
reaching him. Any path to killing him plausibly runs through interrupting that supply,
not through force. The Fates' framing also matters — the possibility exists but requires
his belief, and he has spent the whole season believing.

### P3. What is Hades planning?
> **Settled — [DC9, DC13](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **10**, **14**, **38**.
**Known.** He intends to go back into the Nothing and find a way to restore souls — to
renew them, repair some of the damage, and get them back into the system. He tells Medusa
he has taken too much from humans and is going to defy his brother.

**Missing.** Whether restoration is possible, and what he does now that he has watched a
dead human do it in front of him.

**Constraints.** Hades's principle — the dead do not come back — is already broken twice
in his own house: by his wife releasing Orpheus and by Caeneus renewing a soul. He is
philosophically cornered.

### P4. How organized is the underworld rebellion?
> **Settled — [DC7, DC14, DC37](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **12**, **22**, **29**.
**Known.** Medusa recruits Riddy and names "rebels — people who see the gods for what
they are." Persephone gave the diver order that put Caeneus in place, and unilaterally
released Orpheus as an act of war. Charon takes a phone call about "the man with the
mark" and has been waiting on Prometheus's word for thousands of years.

**Missing.** Whether Medusa's rebels, Persephone's rebellion and Prometheus's plan are
one network or three that happen to overlap. Medusa's line — Riddy was the important one,
there is a plan beyond you — implies she is taking instruction from someone.

**Constraints.** Whoever Medusa answers to knew, in advance, that Riddy mattered more than
Caeneus. That is a very short list of possible sources.

### P5. Is Daedalus alive?
> **Settled — [DC12](11-S2-CANON.md) — he lived.** Runs in **17**.
**Known.** Mauled by Glaucus in Episode 7 when Minos opened the cage remotely. In Episode
8 Ari orders staff to fetch him from the labyrinth.

**Missing.** Any confirmation either way. **Constraints.** He is the only living witness
to Minos's order besides Ari, and the only person who knows Glaucus's care — a survival
or a death both carry weight, so this should be decided deliberately rather than left
vague.

# PART III — OPEN

The real Season 2 workload. Ordered by structural importance.

> **All of these have now been settled for the novelization.** The decisions live
> in [11-S2-CANON.md](11-S2-CANON.md); the constraints below stay put as the audit trail
> those decisions had to satisfy, and are not edited to match them. The last two to close
> were **P1**, the book's final question, answered by [DC29](11-S2-CANON.md) in Chapter 38,
> and **O10**, answered by [DC36](11-S2-CANON.md) in Chapter 11.

### O1. Who is Cassandra working for, and how does she keep finding Riddy?
**Settled — see [D2 in 11-S2-CANON.md](11-S2-CANON.md).**
**Evidence.** E1: arrested for stealing cat food, ranting that the gods steal souls and
that she warned Troy about the horse. She tells Riddy today's the day, that she will
leave everyone, and that her love is dead — and is present again at the crash. E8: waiting
at the exit of the Cave, names herself, tells Riddy she is a prophet now, and issues the
charge: go to Ari and set the living free; Caeneus will do the same for the dead.

**Confirmed.** She is a genuine prophet, cursed to be disbelieved, and every prediction
lands. She knows about Caeneus, whom she has never met and who is dead.

**Inferred.** That she is connected to the same plan as Prometheus and Medusa — nothing
on screen states it.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) Her curse must persist: whatever she says must be
structurally difficult for others to accept, or the character is broken. (b) She knows the
underworld's business from Earth, which requires a source. (c) She hands Riddy the
season's forward mission, so whoever informs Cassandra is effectively directing the human
resistance. (d) She was at Troy, which ties her to Hecuba, Andromache and Nax — a
connection the show sets up and never uses.

### O2. Who visited Prometheus, and whose plan is this?
**Settled — see [D1 in 11-S2-CANON.md](11-S2-CANON.md).**
**Evidence.** E5, before his imprisonment. An unseen speaker knocks, chides him for not
answering the door, tells him it is not his fate to kill Zeus, that he must kill Charon,
that he must regain Zeus's trust and submit to his punishment, and that there is a greater
plan. Prometheus obeys completely.

**Confirmed.** The visitor exists, is trusted absolutely, knows Prometheus's fate, and
knows who Prometheus loves most.

**Inferred.** That it is a Fate. The Fates speak in that register, know fates, and are
revealed in Episode 8 to have been running a longer game than Prometheus knew — telling
him all he had to do was trust and wait, which is exactly the instruction the visitor
gave.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) Whoever it is outranks Prometheus's own judgment and has
been correct for thousands of years. (b) The answer reframes the entire season: if
Prometheus is an instrument, the story's apparent protagonist has been a middle manager.
(c) The Episode 8 reveal — the Fates free him and tell him what he does with his freedom
is up to him — reads as a handover, which implies the plan's author considers its
directed phase complete.

### O3. What does Prometheus do now?
> **Settled — [D1, DC16](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **1**, **24**, **35**, **40**.
**Evidence.** Freed mid-sentence while telling Zeus that his own weakness — his inability
to kill the man he loves — is the only reason Zeus still rules. He then appears inside
Zeus's house and tells him the vision was real and kaos is coming.

**Confirmed.** He is free, physically present on Olympus, and no longer narrating with
authority.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) His stated motive across eight episodes is the destruction
of Zeus, and his stated obstacle is that he loves him. That contradiction is his arc and
must be paid off, not sidestepped. (b) He misses the Earth "more than you could possibly
imagine" — freedom means he can go there. (c) He has been lying to the audience by
omission all season; a novelization has to decide how reliable he now is.

### O4. What do the Fates want?
> **Settled — [DC22](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **25**, **37**.
**Evidence.** They accept their own immolation, choose the room, help Zeus stage it, and
are alive immediately afterward. They free Prometheus and tell him fate can't be
destroyed. They took Zeus's watch as a bribe and let him see it as they burned — which
detonated the Olympus household.

**Confirmed.** They cannot be destroyed by Zeus. They are willing to be bribed, to
manipulate, and to be theatrical about both.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) They never lie; they permit misreadings. Any scene with
them must be technically true. (b) They let Zeus believe he had killed them, which is an
intentional deception by staging rather than by statement. (c) They told Prometheus that
when Caeneus went through the Frame, "the family falls" would happen and Prometheus would
be free — one of those two things demonstrably occurred.

### O5. Is Zeus's belief now broken or hardened?
> **Settled — [DC15, DC21](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **20**, **36**.
**Evidence.** He declares there is no more fate, only loyalty, and that he *is* order. But
he has also had a vision he insists was real, and he has seen his own blood.

**Resolution constraints.** The Fates' rule is explicit: without belief, a prophecy cannot
come to pass. A Zeus who genuinely stopped believing would be safe — which means the
prophecy's completion requires that he cannot stop.

### O6. Why can Caeneus renew souls?
**Settled — see [D3 in 11-S2-CANON.md](11-S2-CANON.md).**
**Evidence.** E5: when Hades extracts Nax, it is *Riddy* who feels it — the ringing hurts
her — while Caeneus feels nothing at all and cannot say why; Prometheus's *he will soon*
lands in E8, when Caeneus gasps at the Frame's ringing. *(Corrected against the
transcripts — an earlier version of this line had the two reversed.)*
E8: he goes into the Lethe after his mother and she comes back. Hades
witnesses it and names it: *you can renew human souls.* Prometheus, watching, expected
Caeneus to be protected and the family to fall, and did not expect this.

**Confirmed.** The act happened, Hades saw it, and nobody involved understands it.

**Inferred.** That Medusa's selection of Caeneus and Persephone's diver order were aimed
at putting him in exactly this position — though Medusa insists Riddy was the important
one.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) This is the single biggest rule-break in the series: it
inverts the entire economy of divinity, since souls in the Nothing are the gods'
inventory. (b) It happened at the moment of a soul's passage and involved his mother
specifically — the mechanism may require love, consent, or contact, and any answer should
address why it has never happened before. (c) Hades wanted precisely this ability and was
about to search the Nothing for it; the two of them now need each other.

### O7. What is Riddy's prophecy now?
> **Settled — [DC16](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **28**, **40**.
**Evidence.** She refuses to share her reading all season and then discovers it is
identical to Caeneus's. Cassandra tells her she is a prophet now.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) Her original prophecy is arguably fulfilled — she left
everyone, and the family is falling. (b) "So are you now" implies transfer or succession,
which the show has never depicted. (c) The Fates write prophecies, and the Fates are
supposedly dead, so any new prophecy has to be reconciled with Zeus's belief that he
abolished fate.

### O8. What becomes of Orpheus?
> **Settled — [DC11, DC20](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **5**, **19**, **39**.
**Evidence.** He is the only living human to have entered the underworld and returned. He
committed murder to do it. He learned his wife was leaving him the day she died, released
her, and drove away alone. Hades knows his name. Dionysus loves him. Persephone likes his
music and offered to visit his mother.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) He is currently useless to every faction and known to all
of them, which makes him either a weapon or a casualty. (b) His guilt over the coin and
over Anatole is unresolved and unpunished. (c) He is the one character who can testify
about the underworld to the living and is not under anyone's orders.

### O9. What is Hera building?
> **Settled — [DC17](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **2**, **26**, **33**.
**Evidence.** Confined by Zeus, stripped of her bees and her leverage. She immediately
phones someone, calls herself "Mommy," and instructs them to gather the troops and make up
a spare bed. The subtitles place a `[coin clinks]` cue directly after the call — possibly
Hera palming a dead-passage coin **(inference — a sound cue only)**.

**Confirmed.** She has an ally with troops and a household, whom she addresses as a
parent.

**Inferred.** Nothing safely. She has no children shown on screen; the endearment is
ambiguous.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) Whoever it is must be able to house a fugitive queen and
raise a force, which points at the absent Olympians named in Episode 2 or at some power
outside Zeus's household. (b) Hera refused Poseidon's offer to run away on the grounds that
Olympus is *her* house — so any plan she runs is about taking it, not leaving it. (c) She
built her influence on the tacitas' confessions, and whether she can still draw on them
while confined is unestablished. *[Corrected against the transcripts: an earlier version
of this constraint said she had lost the tacitas as a power source. Nothing in E8
establishes that — Zeus confines her on Olympus, where the tongue cabinet is, and his
seizures name cities, not tacitas; [06-OLYMPUS.md](06-OLYMPUS.md) has this right. That
she walks north without a single tacita is a canon decision ([DC17](11-S2-CANON.md)),
not S1 fact.]*

### O10. Poseidon, dispossessed and in love
> **Settled — [DC26, DC36](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **11** and **34**. He is *for* nothing, and the year is the demonstration of it.
**Evidence.** Stripped of Krete and every city, told to grow up. He has admitted to
Dionysus that he has loved Hera forever and that it feels like his heart is outside his
body. Dionysus promised not to tell, and kept the promise.

**Resolution constraints.** He is the most self-interested god in the series and has just
lost everything self-interest could protect. His alliance is available to whoever asks
first, and Hera is the obvious buyer.

### O11. Dionysus and Troy
> **Settled — [DC10, DC35](11-S2-CANON.md); Nax's non-restoration settled separately by
> [DC40](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **8**, **39**.
**Evidence.** He asks the Trojans what they want and promises to do it — *What do you
want? Tell me, and I'll do it.* They want Troy rebuilt and Olympus destroyed. The scene
carries no speaker tags; **Nax is not in it** (dead since Olympia night, extracted by Hades
in E5, and the name does not occur after E5), and that the survivors present are Hecuba and
Andromache is **(inference)**. Constraint (a) below was right and the evidence line was
wrong; corrected against the transcripts. He has just watched his father murder his
cat to teach him that love is weakness, and been told by Prometheus that maybe the better
part of him is human. He has also declared, watching Ari kill Minos, that he thinks he's
in love.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) Nax is dead — killed by the Minotaur — so the Trojans
Dionysus is helping have lost their prince, and Dionysus was present at neither event.
Whether he knows is unestablished. (b) A god can bring water, weather, and Meander access;
he cannot bring the dead back, and he has been told so by everyone. (c) His declared love
for Ari has not been acted on and Ari does not know he exists.

### O12. What does Ari do with Krete?
> **Settled — [DC10, DC12, DC18, DC24, DC34](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **3**, **6**, **9**, **18**, **21**, **39**.
**Evidence.** She killed the president, refused to rule for the gods, and told Riddy's
world nothing yet. Her mother approves. Glaucus is dead — Minos knifed him in the
labyrinth minutes after she unmasked him — and she has his body and thirty years of
waxworks of a brother she was told she had smothered. Cassandra has dispatched Riddy
to her.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) Krete is Poseidon's former possession and now Zeus's
direct one — Zeus announced he is taking charge of every city. A publicly apostate ruler
of Krete is a direct provocation. (b) The Trojans in Krete are the obvious constituency
for a ruler who has broken with Olympus, and Ari has already freed them once. (c) She
did not save Glaucus and reached him only in time to watch him killed; whatever she
does with Krete is done by someone carrying that, and the show's cruelty about
consequences forbids resolving it cleanly.

### O13. The Nothing, and the structural collapse
> **Settled — [DC13](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **27**, **30**, **38**.
**Evidence.** Hades: the Nothing was never meant to take that many; the Frame glitched
because of the strain; it could break, and not just the underworld — Olympus and Earth
too. Zeus's response was to forbid any change and strike him down.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) This is a stated ticking clock and the show never walks it
back. (b) Hades's proposed fix — restoring souls to Earth — is exactly what Caeneus just
demonstrated. (c) Zeus has now taken direct charge of the City of the Dead, which puts the
failing system under the management of the one god who denies it is failing.

### O14. The unresolved dead
> **Settled — [DC14, DC37](11-S2-CANON.md) — the Stoppage.** Runs in **12**, **29**.
**Evidence.** Riddy's cohort was sentenced to two hundred years. Prue and Adrian have
their own waits. Caeneus has served ten. Fotis is still at the port.

**Resolution constraints.** If there is no Renewal, the two-hundred-year sentence was
always meaningless, and the unresolved are the only population in the underworld with
nothing to lose. They are a standing army nobody has recruited.

### O15. Zeus's absent children
> **Settled — [DC5, DC17](11-S2-CANON.md).** Runs in **2**, **26**, **32**.
**Evidence.** Ares, Athena, Apollo, Aphrodite, Artemis and Hermes are named on answering
machines in Episode 2. None answers. Zeus's own theory is that his children disappoint him
and so he keeps making more. Hera has been killing the mortal ones at birth.

**Resolution constraints.** Their absence is established as deliberate estrangement, not
non-existence. Any appearance must account for six gods who chose to stop answering the
phone — and, given O9, for who might be raising troops.

### O17. The Furies
> **Settled — [DC39](11-S2-CANON.md) — released, with the reason on record.**

*(Added in the 2026-08 transcript audit: the season leaves this faction unaccounted for,
and no earlier entry covered it.)*

**Evidence.** E3: Alecto and Tisiphone drive a man named Terry to shoot himself in a
gas-station bathroom — "you know how to get rid of us" — then pick their next target.
Prometheus's gloss: as old as the gods, or older; delivering justice where they feel it
has been denied, "quite subjective"; visible and audible only to their target. Theseus's
gloss: they come for murderers and, crucially, for people who go unpunished — and not for
those who have punished themselves. E5: with Megaera, they hold Ari not to punish her but
to show her the truth, tell her to fuck the gods, and close out a favor with the Fates —
"we're even now, no more favors" — with a Fate present (see the favor note in
[01-CHARACTERS.md](01-CHARACTERS.md)).

**Confirmed.** They exist outside the Olympian hierarchy, predate it, act autonomously,
and were used — once — as the instrument that put the third of Prometheus's three humans
in motion.

**Resolution constraints.** (a) Their trigger is justice *denied*, and the season ends
thick with enormous unpunished acts — Zeus's above all — so any continuation owes their
absence a stated reason, not silence. (b) Only their target perceives them, which caps
how visibly they can ever act. (c) The favor with the Fates is closed; nothing obliges
either side to the other again.

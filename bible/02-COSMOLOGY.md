# Cosmology — How This Universe Actually Works

The rules of the world, with the episode each rule is established in. Anything the
show implies but never states outright is marked **(inference)**.

---

## 1. Divinity is manufactured, not innate

**Zeus was born mortal.** Prometheus says it flatly to the audience at the end of
Episode 1: Zeus was not always a god, he didn't create humanity, he used to be one of
them — *which is why he can be destroyed by them.*

**The mechanism (E6).** Zeus hated his father Kronos, and after years of torment he
killed him — violently, passionately. At the moment of death, Zeus absorbed his
father's soul. It became part of him. It gave him power, and the power was
intoxicating, so he did it again. And again. It took him years to work out how he had
done it in the first place.

**Industrialization (E6).** Eventually Zeus tired of the killing, and the Frame was
invented — *not* to renew human souls but to harvest them, to liquidize them. The
process was industrialized and sanitized. The gods drink the product to stay immortal.

**Therefore the lie is load-bearing.** The myths must be maintained, the humans
distracted, the story controlled. Prometheus names distraction as the thing Zeus
relies on above all.

### Meander water
The liquid form of harvested souls. It flows through Olympus as a river-structure —
"a divine structure," per Prometheus: as long as it flows, Zeus reigns. Zeus badgers
Prometheus to drink his ration through a novelty bendy straw in nearly every scene
they share. Suckling from the Meander is how an outsider is *made* divine: Hera
accuses Zeus of planning to let a lover drink from it and turn them into his little
helper. In Episode 8 Zeus turns the supply into a leash, issuing one rationed bottle
per family member — and none at all for Hera.

### What gods can and cannot do
- **Gods don't bleed.** Dionysus says it when Orpheus shoots him. In Episode 8 Zeus
  dreams of bleeding, and a Fate says the line back to him.
- Gods can be present on Earth unseen; humans only perceive them if the god chooses.
- Gods can transform (Hera's bees), kill instantly, teleport, and manipulate weather.
- Gods can be hurt by each other — Zeus's lightning nearly kills Hades.
- **Gods cannot bring back the dead.** Hades states it as sacred law. It is the one
  prohibition treated as inviolable by everyone except Persephone, who breaks it as an
  act of war.
- **A human can renew a soul.** Caeneus does it in Episode 8, in front of Hades, and
  neither of them understands how. This is the single largest rule-break in the series.

## 2. Death, and the machinery of it

Full operational detail in [07-UNDERWORLD.md](07-UNDERWORLD.md). The metaphysics:

**The coin.** Each mortal has a coin bearer who buries them with a coin, and the coin
is what buys passage through the Frame. Without one, the dead cannot pass — as
Prometheus puts it, the water simply will not rise to meet them. They are *unresolved*
and are told they must wait two hundred years for another chance.

**The rivers.** The **Styx** carries the dead from Earth to the underworld by ferry.
The **Lethe** is where the actual crossing happens; divers guide souls into the water
and through the Frame.

**Free will.** No one can be forced into the water or through the Frame. Medusa says
so explicitly during diver training — free will is inefficient, which is exactly why
divers exist. Persuasion is the entire job.

**The Frame.** Hades built it on Zeus's orders. The orientation video sells it as a
joyful return of one's atoms to the Earth. It is a harvester.

**The Nothing.** Where processed souls go. A vast dim space full of standing, ruined
people. Riddy and Caeneus find it in Episode 4. Souls in it appear to persist in some
degraded form rather than being annihilated outright — which is precisely what makes
Hades's plan to restore them thinkable **(inference: the show never states what a soul
in the Nothing still is)**.

**Structural failure.** The Nothing was never designed for this volume. Overload made
the Frame glitch, which let Nax escape unfinished — the first time in history, per
Hades. Hades warns that souls are not being returned to Earth, that the system cannot
disintegrate what it holds, that it will eventually break, and that when it does it
takes the underworld, Olympus and Earth with it. Zeus's answer is that you cannot run
out of souls, because he is king of the gods.

**Manual extraction.** Hades can finish the job by hand, by touch. Caeneus physically
feels it happen as a piercing pain; Riddy standing beside him feels nothing at all.
Neither of them knows why. **(Open — see [05-MYSTERIES.md](05-MYSTERIES.md).)**

## 3. Prophecy

**Only humans get prophecies.** Prometheus says it directly; it is also the crack in
Zeus's confidence, because Zeus has one.

**The Fates write them.** They write so many that they cannot always remember a
specific one and have to look it up. They also set the questions and keep the score at
the Cave — "on several levels," per Dionysus.

**Prophecies are precise and merciless, and the reading is the danger.** Minos's
prophecy — *"Your end begins in the marital bed. The first child to draw breath will
kill you dead"* — was accurate. He assumed "first to draw breath" meant the firstborn.
It meant the twin who came out screaming. He murdered the wrong child, tortured the
other for thirty years, and was killed by the daughter he raised. When Poseidon asks
the Fates whether Minos can kill his son, they answer truthfully: he can. They are not
lying. Everyone else supplies the error.

**Defiance is possible, and it is a trap.** The Fates tell Poseidon that people can
occasionally defy their prophecies. They tell Zeus in Episode 8 that for a prophecy to
come to pass he must bring it into being: the possibility exists, but without his
belief it cannot. Zeus's entire season is a man manufacturing his own prophecy by
sprinting away from it.

**Fate cannot be destroyed.** Zeus burns the three Fates alive in Episode 8 in a room
of their choosing. They are alive immediately afterward, unbothered, freeing
Prometheus and telling him all he had to do was trust — and wait.

### The shared prophecy
> **"A line appears, the order wanes, the family falls, and kaos reigns."**

Given, identically, to **Zeus**, **Riddy**, **Ari**, and **Caeneus**. Prometheus tells
the audience in Episode 1 that no two prophecies are ever the same — *supposedly.*
Caeneus and Riddy discover the duplication in Episode 6 and treat it as impossible.

Each holder has read it as a private indictment:
- **Zeus** — the wrinkle, the blasphemy on the monument, the collapse of his family.
- **Ari** — she thought she fulfilled it before the age of two by killing her twin.
- **Caeneus** — he crossed a line, changed his body, changed the order, ruined his
  family, brought chaos. His mother's own prophecy foretold that he would be a boy.
- **Riddy** — never states her private reading; refuses to share it for most of the
  season.

Riddy and Caeneus reach the correct reading together in Episode 6: **the family that
falls is the gods.**

## 4. Older powers

**The Furies** are as old as the gods, or older. They roam the Earth delivering
justice where they judge it has been denied — which is subjective. Only their target
can see or hear them. Their method is psychological: they wear a person down until the
person kills themselves. They trade in favors, including with the Fates.

**The Fates** predate and outrank the question. Their Episode 8 self-description to
Zeus: they are everywhere, outside him looking in and inside him looking out; they are
him; and also they are nothing to do with him.

**The Cave.** A venue on Earth, entered through a bin, where the desperate compete for
access to the underworld. Publicly a scam; actually a functioning door. Entry fee, an
audience, and a quiz whose rounds are cruel and specific — mark every freckle and
blemish on your loved one; tell us about your son without speaking; sing of your love
without your voice. The Fates judge. Winning entry is only the beginning: there are
tests below, ending with Hades himself, who is not obliged to say yes.

**Monsters.** The **Scylla** in the Styx. **Charybdis** in the water, which nearly
takes Orpheus. Working **cerberus dogs** at the port, which are not to be petted and
which can smell a coinless soul.

**The mark.** A sign given by a Fate, worn on the hand. Prometheus made Charon swear,
before killing him, to protect a stranger he would recognize by the mark. Orpheus
carries it; Charon honors the oath. Orpheus is told only that it means *something is
true*.

**The tacitas.** Women who cut out their tongues in service to Hera. Humans confess
into their booths believing it is a devotion; the confessions are kept by Hera, and
they give her power and soothe her heart. Their vow of silence is to her; their tongues
are hers, stored in a cabinet of drawers, and can be replayed. Zeus is forbidden by
oath to touch them, breaks the oath, listens, and is nearly destroyed by what he thinks
he hears. Riddy's mother is one.

## 5. Standing rules for Season 2 consistency

1. Every god's immortality depends on a continuously replenished supply of harvested
   human souls. Cut the supply and the gods age.
2. Zeus's power is not native. It is accumulated, and at least once he has dreamed of
   losing it in the most literal way — blood.
3. Prophecy is real, unbreakable in content, and lethally ambiguous in reading. The
   Fates never lie; they simply do not correct anyone.
4. Belief is the activating mechanism. A prophecy denied belief cannot come to pass.
5. Nobody can be forced through the Frame. Persuasion is the whole system, which means
   the whole system is vulnerable to a counter-persuasion.
6. The dead do not come back — except that Persephone sent one back, and Caeneus
   renewed one, both in the last two episodes.
